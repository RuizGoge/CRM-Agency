# 9. Lead Prioritization — "Who do I call next?" (within-silo, no routing)

> **Module id:** `scoring-priority` · part of [`docs/02-functional-map.md`](../02-functional-map.md)

## Verdict
RESHAPE — keep it as a module, but strip it of vanity surface area. It must ship as (1) a headless **priority engine** (transparent, versioned, weighted-factor scoring + a hard compliance gate) that every other module reads, and (2) exactly ONE seller-facing work surface: the **Call Next queue** (with its "Why this lead?" drawer, snooze/pin list, and admin model settings). It must NOT ship a "scoring dashboard", a lead grade letter, or a second kanban. Rationale: the ranking has to exist somewhere — a seller with 300 leads in a silo, buying FE leads that decay in minutes, cannot answer "who next?" from a kanban board sorted by stage — but a score nobody acts on is dead weight, so the score's only job is to ORDER work surfaces that already exist (kanban badge, My Day list, mobile "Now" card, Aloware Power Dialer list). Concretely: pipeline-kanban renders the priority badge but does not compute it; tasks/my-day renders the ordered list but does not own the ordering; dialer-integration pushes the queue but does not build it. Explicit non-goals restated so no one re-adds them: NO cross-seller comparison of leads, NO "best rep for this lead", NO round-robin, NO queue that any user other than the owner can pull from. If Phase 3 decides the engine is too much for MVP, the correct cut is to degrade to a two-factor ordering (compliance gate + freshness/recency) still owned by THIS module — not to dissolve the module into pipeline, because the compliance gate and the ping-post golden-window clock have no other natural owner.

## Purpose & value
It answers the only question a seller asks 60 times a day — "which of MY leads do I call right now?" — by ranking their own book on freshness, replies, promises made, stage urgency and stale risk, while silently hiding anyone it would be illegal or pointless to dial right now.

## Screens & states (5)

### Call Next (priority queue)
The seller's ordered, compliance-gated list of their own leads to work right now — the module's single primary surface.

**Key actions:** `Call now (one tap, Aloware)` · `Send SMS / email (consent-gated)` · `Open lead timeline` · `Snooze until…` · `Pin to top` · `Skip with reason` · `Filter by tier / product track / stage / source` · `Start Power Dialer session with top N` · `Open 'Why this lead?'`

- **Empty:** Headline: "Nothing to call right now." Body: "Your queue fills automatically the moment a lead comes in, replies, or promises you a callback. Add your first lead to get started — new purchased leads jump straight to the top." Primary button: "Add a lead". Secondary link: "See snoozed & suppressed (7)". Variant when everything is suppressed by the calling window: "All 12 of your leads are outside their legal calling window. The next one becomes callable at 9:00 AM ET (in 47 min)."
- **Loading:** Skeleton rows (6) preserving row height so the list does not jump; the header shows "Ranking your book…". If scores are older than the freshness threshold, rows render immediately from the cached score with a subtle "Updating…" pill rather than blocking the list.
- **Error:** Inline banner: "We couldn't rank your leads just now. Showing your most recent leads first so you can keep calling." Degrades to recency ordering with the compliance gate STILL enforced (never fail open on consent/DNC/window). Retry link + error id. If the compliance data itself is unavailable, the queue shows only leads with a verified-callable cached state and says: "Some leads are hidden until we can re-check consent."
- **No permission:** Reached via a deep link to another seller's queue: "This queue belongs to another seller. Leads and queues are private to their owner." Supervisors/admins instead see the read-only variant with a header chip: "Viewing Maria R.'s queue — read only. You can't call, snooze, or pin from here."
- **Mobile:** Default mobile view is the single 'Now' card (one lead, one reason, one big Call button) with swipe-left to snooze, swipe-right to skip; the full list is one tap away. Local time of the lead and the consent chip are always visible above the fold. Call action must survive poor connectivity: the tap is queued and confirmed.

### Why this lead? (priority inspector drawer)
Explain the rank in plain English so the seller trusts and can argue with the order.

**Key actions:** `Expand factor breakdown` · `See suppression reason and callable-at time` · `Report 'this ranking is wrong' (typified feedback)` · `Jump to the lead timeline` · `Override: pin / snooze`

- **Empty:** For a lead with no signals yet: "Not much to go on yet. This lead was created 3 minutes ago from Vendor A and hasn't been called. Freshness is the only reason it's at the top — and it's the best reason there is right now."
- **Loading:** Drawer opens instantly with the lead header and score already known; factor rows fade in individually. Never a full-drawer spinner.
- **Error:** "We can't show the breakdown for this lead right now. Its rank still comes from the last successful calculation (14 min ago)." Shows cached factors greyed out with the timestamp.
- **No permission:** Not reachable for non-owners except in supervisor read-only mode, where override buttons are hidden with the tooltip: "Only the lead's owner can pin or snooze."
- **Mobile:** Opens as a bottom sheet at 60% height; the top three factors are visible without scrolling.

### Snoozed & Suppressed
Show everything deliberately kept out of the queue and why, so absence is never mistaken for loss — and so compliance suppression is auditable.

**Key actions:** `Un-snooze / bring back now` · `Filter by reason (snoozed, no consent, DNC, opted out, outside window, cold, closed)` · `View consent certificate` · `Request re-check of DNC/consent` · `Start manual recycle of a cold lead`

- **Empty:** "Nothing is being held back. Every lead you own is either in your queue or already closed."
- **Loading:** Grouped skeletons by reason with the group counts loading first (counts come from the cached projection).
- **Error:** "We couldn't load the suppression list. Your queue is unaffected — suppressed leads stay suppressed." Retry + error id.
- **No permission:** Sellers see only their own. Supervisors/admins see a tenant-wide read-only view grouped by seller with a header: "Read-only oversight view. Un-snoozing is up to the lead's owner."
- **Mobile:** Reason groups collapse by default; consent certificate opens as a document preview, never a download.

### Priority Model Settings (admin)
Tune and version the scoring model, decay curves, golden window, cold threshold and tier bands for the tenant.

**Key actions:** `Edit factor weights` · `Edit ping-post golden window and decay curve` · `Edit cold-after-days` · `Edit tier bands` · `Enable/disable product-track profiles (FE / IUL)` · `Preview reorder against today's live data` · `Activate new version` · `Roll back to a previous version` · `View change history`

- **Empty:** First run, before any tuning: "You're using the default Life priority model. It puts brand-new purchased leads, replies and promised callbacks first — that's usually right. Preview how a change would reorder today's queues before you turn it on." Primary button: "Preview the default model".
- **Loading:** Form renders from the active version immediately; the preview panel shows "Re-ranking a sample of 200 live leads…" with a progress indicator, and is the only blocked region.
- **Error:** "We couldn't save this version. Nothing changed — sellers are still on version 4." Save is atomic; a failed activation never leaves a half-applied model.
- **No permission:** Supervisors: read-only with "Model settings are admin-only. You can see the current weights but not change them." Sellers: the screen is not in the navigation at all.
- **Mobile:** Read-only on mobile — weight editing and preview require desktop. Mobile shows the active version summary.

### Speed-to-Lead & Priority Health (supervisor/admin)
Oversight of the one behavior that decides FE conversion — how fast new leads get their first call — plus whether the model is calibrated.

**Key actions:** `View median/p90 time-to-first-attempt by seller, source and hour` · `View % of purchased leads called inside the golden window` · `View close rate by priority tier (calibration)` · `View queue adherence per seller` · `View compliance blocks (count by reason)` · `Drill into a specific missed-window lead`

- **Empty:** "No leads have come in yet. Once leads start arriving, you'll see how fast each seller makes the first call — the number that moves Final Expense conversion more than anything else."
- **Loading:** KPI tiles load first with their own skeletons; charts stream in after. Time-range selector stays interactive throughout.
- **Error:** "We couldn't load speed-to-lead metrics for this range. Try a shorter range or reload." Partial data is labeled "Partial — 2 of 9 days missing" rather than silently averaged.
- **No permission:** Sellers reaching this screen see their OWN speed-to-lead only, with: "You can see your own speed-to-lead here. Team-wide numbers are for supervisors and admins." — never other sellers' figures (the leaderboard is the only cross-seller comparison surface).
- **Mobile:** Tiles stack single-column; charts collapse to sparklines with a tap-to-expand full view; the seller drill-down table scrolls horizontally in its own container.

| `lead.imported` | Score in bulk with the freshness factor neutralized — an imported 6-month-old list must never masquerade as fresh ping-post leads at the top of the queue. |
| `call.completed` | Reset recency, increment attempt/connect counters, apply the disposition's scoring effect (connected / no answer / voicemail / bad number / do-not-call), set time_to_first_attempt_seconds if unset, and scan the AloAi summary for a callback promise suggestion. |
| `call.inbound_received` | Escalate to Call now with a short urgency window — a lead calling back is the strongest buying signal in the system — and record it as engagement, not as an attempt. |
| `call.missed` | Same escalation as an inbound call plus a 'they tried to reach you' reason on the card; never counted as a seller attempt. |
| `call.disposition_set` | Apply disposition weights, damp or hard-suppress on 'do not call' / 'wrong number', and feed the best-time histogram with the outcome for that hour-of-week. |
| `sms.received` | Escalate to Call now with a decaying urgency window and record inbound engagement. |
| `sms.sent` | Reset recency weakly (an outbound touch is not engagement) and increment the channel attempt counter for fatigue damping. |
| `email.received` | Escalate as inbound engagement (weight below SMS/call, since email replies are slower-intent in this vertical). |
| `email.sent` | Reset recency weakly; email opens/clicks are deliberately NOT consumed as scoring signals. |
| `note.created` | Reset the recency clock and stale countdown — manual work counts as a touch. |
| `opportunity.stage_changed` | Re-weight stage urgency; entering a closed stage (won or lost) removes the lead from the queue immediately and stops the stale clock. |
| `opportunity.value_changed` | Recompute the expected-value factor (deal_value = annual premium). |
| `opportunity.created` | Bind the score row to the opportunity and apply the product-track profile (FE vs IUL) from its pipeline type. |
| `meeting.scheduled` | Suppress from the call queue until the pre-meeting prep window opens, then re-surface with a 'confirm the appointment' reason. |
| `meeting.no_show` | Immediately re-escalate to Call now with a short high-urgency rebound window and the reason 'no-show — call now'. |
| `meeting.completed` | Reset recency and hand priority back to stage urgency (post-meeting follow-up). |
| `meeting.canceled` | Return the lead to the normal queue with recency intact and raise urgency modestly (a cancel is a soft objection that decays fast). |
| `task.completed` | Reset recency if the task was a contact attempt; clear the pin if it fulfilled a callback promise. |
| `lead.went_cold` | Drop to the Nurture tier, clear the stale warning, and evaluate lead.recycle_suggested heuristics. This module does NOT own the cold flag — it consumes it and stops warning about it. |
| `contact.opted_out` | Hard-suppress the affected channel permanently (or until a new verified consent), emit lead.contact_suppressed, and disenroll from any dialer list — score is irrelevant once consent is gone. |
| `contact.dnc_flagged` | Hard-suppress all outbound calling immediately with an evidence reference. We mirror Aloware's DNC state; we never contradict it or maintain a competing list. |
| `consent.updated` | Re-evaluate the compliance gate and emit lead.contact_unsuppressed if the lead becomes callable; store the certificate reference for the audit trail. |
| `sequence.enrolled` | Damp manual-call priority for leads under an active automated cadence so the seller is not dialing someone the sequence is about to text. |
| `sequence.paused` | Restore manual priority — a sequence pauses because the lead replied or booked, which is exactly when a human should call. |
| `pipeline.stage_config_changed` | Re-read stage urgency and closed-stage flags, then recompute all affected scores in the tenant. |
| `user.availability_changed` | Rebuild the queue against the seller's working hours so leads are not surfaced when the seller cannot call, without ever overriding the lead's legal calling window. |

## Typical automations
- When a new purchased lead is created → put it at the top of my Call Next queue, push a mobile notification, and keep it flagged 'call now' for 5 minutes.
- When 5 minutes pass and a new purchased lead still has no call attempt → escalate the notification; at 30 minutes → emit lead.freshness_expired so the owner's speed-to-lead report catches it.
- When a lead replies by SMS or email → jump the lead to the top of my queue for 15 minutes and pause any running sequence.
- When a lead calls me back or I miss their call → put them at the top with the reason 'they called you' and offer one-tap call back.
- When an appointment is no-showed → put the lead back at the top for 15 minutes with 'no-show — call now'.
- When a call summary mentions a callback time → suggest a callback promise for one-tap confirmation; on confirm, create the task and pin the lead at that time.
- When a confirmed callback time passes without an attempt → notify me immediately and mark the promise missed.
- When a lead has had no activity for 6 days → raise the stale warning on the kanban card and add it to My Day.
- When a lead's local time is outside 8am-9pm → hide it from the queue and show 'Callable at 9:00 AM ET'; bring it back automatically when the window opens.
- When a lead opts out or matches DNC → remove it from every queue and dialer list permanently and log the block with evidence.
- When a lead is enrolled in an automated sequence → lower its manual-call priority until the sequence pauses or ends.
- When 6 unanswered attempts happen within 14 days → drop the lead to Nurture and suggest an SMS/email cadence instead of another dial.
- When I start a calling session → push my top 25 to the Aloware Power Dialer list and freeze their order until the session ends.
- When a lead's stage moves to a Closed stage → remove it from my queue immediately.
- When a cold lead's dormancy and loss reason match the recycle heuristics → suggest it for manual re-engagement (never auto-contact).
- When an admin activates a new priority model version → recompute all scores and tell sellers 'Your queue order was updated' rather than silently reshuffling.

## KPIs
- Median and p90 time-to-first-attempt on new purchased leads (speed-to-lead) — the module's north star
- % of ping-post leads with a first call attempt inside the golden window (target: the number owners actually buy this product for)
- Queue adherence: % of outbound calls placed on a lead that was in the seller's top 10 at the time of the call
- Contact rate of queue-followed calls vs off-queue calls (the proof the ranking beats scrolling)
- Tier calibration: close rate and average annual premium by priority tier (Call now > Today > This week > Nurture, or the model is noise)
- Callback promise honor rate: % of confirmed promises called within ±15 minutes
- Stale rescue rate: % of leads that received a touch after a stale warning and before going cold
- Compliance integrity: calls placed outside the legal calling window or to DNC/no-consent leads — target is exactly zero, measured, not assumed
- Suppression transparency: % of suppressed leads whose reason was viewed at least once (low = sellers think leads vanished)
- Override pressure: % of queue snoozed/skipped per seller per day (a rising number means the model is wrong, not that sellers are lazy)
- Median score-recompute latency from triggering event (p95 target: single-digit seconds for escalation events)
- Days-to-first-touch distribution for imported/non-purchased leads

## Insurance specifics (Life / FE / IUL) & vertical-agnostic core
Genuinely Life/FE/IUL-specific here, and nowhere else in the system: (1) **Ping-post minute-level decay** — purchased Final Expense leads are sold to several agencies simultaneously and go dead in minutes, so freshness must be a seconds-resolution curve, not the day-resolution 'lead age' every generic CRM ships. (2) **TCPA/DNC as a hard gate rather than a scoring penalty** — US outbound life telesales is the highest-liability calling context in the product; the queue must fail CLOSED, carry the vendor consent certificate from intake into every dial, and respect the 8am-9pm window in the LEAD's time zone (derived from phone NPA with state/ZIP fallback, with the derivation source recorded because it is legally material). (3) **FE vs IUL are two different sports** — FE is one-call-close telesales where connect speed decides everything and a 3-day-old lead is nearly worthless; IUL is a consultative multi-touch sale where 'no touch for 5 days' is normal and stage progress (illustration delivered, application signed, underwriting requirement outstanding) drives urgency. A single decay curve mis-scores both. (4) **Near-money underwriting chase** — simplified-issue FE and IUL cases sit in Application/Underwriting waiting on a signature, a voice-verification call, or a health requirement; these outrank new leads because they are one action from Earnings, and no generic 'lead score' models that. (5) **Demographic contactability reality** — FE prospects are largely retirees on landlines with predictable answer windows, and FE draft dates are commonly aligned to Social Security deposit timing, which makes best-time-to-call and promise timing far more predictive here than in B2B. (6) **Lead-vendor quality variance** — FE vendor lists differ by multiples in contact and close rate, so vendor quality is a first-class scoring input, not a reporting curiosity. How the CORE stays vertical-agnostic: the engine knows nothing about insurance. It evaluates a generic ordered pipeline of named **factor plugins** over entity-agnostic signals (recency, inbound engagement, stage urgency from the pipeline config, expected value from deal_value, attempt fatigue, promise pressure, source quality) plus a pluggable **contactability gate** whose rules are configuration, not code. Everything above ships as a swappable 'Life / FE / IUL factor pack' bound to a pipeline template and a product-track profile: the golden window, the decay curve, the calling-window rule, the underwriting-urgency stage weights and the vendor scorecard are all rows in priority_model_config, versioned per tenant. Adding a second vertical (e.g. Medicare, P&C, or a non-insurance line) means authoring a new factor pack and profile — never touching the scoring engine, the queue screen, or a single event name.

## ⚔️ Adversarial review (sales-ops critic)

**Verdict:** NEEDS WORK — the instincts are right (one queue, a hard fail-closed compliance gate, a minute-level ping-post clock, no routing), but it ships a data-science scoring apparatus where an FE floor needs a deterministic rule ladder, and it is missing the entire money side of life insurance: multi-number dialing, draft dates, NSF/lapse save calls, chargeback risk, and a real underwriting-requirement chase. An agency owner will not buy it because it optimizes the 20% of the day that is fresh leads, leaves the aged-lead power-dialer grind to V1.1, and has no answer for a $35 lead landing on a seller who is at lunch.

### Must fix — missing for real operation (12)
- MULTIPLE PHONE NUMBERS PER LEAD — the single biggest operational miss. FE vendor leads arrive with 1-3 numbers (landline, cell, spouse/alt) and aged lists are full of disconnects. This spec models contactability, DNC, opt-out, attempts and 'contact_phone_e164' at the LEAD level. In reality all of it is NUMBER-level: one number can be DNC while another is clean, 'bad number' kills a number not a lead, and the queue must say 'try the cell, the landline is disconnected.' Without number-level attempt/suppression state the compliance gate is both over-suppressive (kills a whole lead for one bad number) and under-suppressive (dials a DNC alt number). This alone is a redesign of lead_contactability_state and lead_attempt_stats.
- THE ENTIRE POST-SALE MONEY LAYER IS ABSENT. FE commission is ADVANCED and charged back if the policy lapses in the first 9-12 months or if the first draft NSFs. The highest-value call an FE agent makes all week is a SAVE CALL on a failed first draft / pending lapse / policy-issued-not-delivered. None of it exists here: no policy entity reference, no draft_date, no first-draft-failed event, no lapse-pending signal, no free-look window. A priority queue that ranks a brand-new $2 lead above a $1,400 AP policy about to chargeback is not a life-insurance queue. Note this also exposes an Earnings-model problem the module should raise: Closed-Won counts premium that may be clawed back, and this module owns the only clock that could warn about it.
- DRAFT DATE / SOCIAL SECURITY DEPOSIT TIMING is name-dropped in insurance_specifics and then modeled nowhere. FE draft dates are commonly aligned to SSA deposit schedule (2nd/3rd/4th Wednesday by birth date). This drives (a) when to call to close ('your draft would come out the 3rd Wednesday'), (b) when to call before a draft to prevent an NSF, (c) best-time-to-reach. It should be a first-class field and a first-class priority rung. As written it is a sentence in a paragraph.
- THE UNDERWRITING CHASE HAS NO DATA SOURCE. 'Near-money first' is declared a factor and justified beautifully, but the only input is stage_id. Real requirements are: unsigned application, voice-verification/PHI interview not completed, APS outstanding, carrier requirement letter, payment info missing, policy issued-awaiting-delivery. There is no entity, no field, no consumed event (nothing like requirement.added / policy.issued / application.signature_pending), and no carrier or agency-back-office integration in scope. Either define an owner-editable 'outstanding requirement + due date' field on the opportunity and own the chase clock, or admit the factor is decorative. Currently it is decorative.
- THE AGED-LEAD GRIND — the actual 80% of an FE seller's day — is not in MVP. Speed-to-lead on fresh ping-post leads is the glamour case; the daily reality is a seller sitting down at 9am with 250 aged leads and no fresh ones, working a power-dialer session for three hours. This spec makes the Aloware Power Dialer list push mvp_candidate:false. That means MVP ships a nicer list and the seller still hand-dials the grind. For an FE floor that is the difference between 40 and 150 attempts a day. Power Dialer push is not V1.1 — it IS the product for hours 2 through 6 of the day.
- NO ANSWER TO THE SILO'S CENTRAL BUSINESS PROBLEM: what happens to a ping-post lead that lands on a seller who is at lunch, on a call, or offline? With no routing and no reassignment, a $35 exclusive FE lead can sit for 90 minutes and die. The spec emits lead.freshness_expired into a report and calls it handled. An owner buying $3-8k/month of leads will ask this in the first demo and 'it shows up in a report tomorrow' loses the sale. The silo-legal answer must be designed and stated: seller availability/presence, vendor-side ping rejection when the owner is unavailable, aggressive multi-channel escalation to the owner (push + SMS to the seller's own phone), and a supervisor ALERT (not a reassignment) at N minutes.
- VOICEMAIL STRATEGY. Aloware supports voicemail drop; FE floors live on it. The queue must decide and record: drop VM #1 on attempt 1, no VM on 2-3, drop VM #2 on attempt 4. A VM drop is not a connect and should not reset recency the same way. Nothing in the spec distinguishes VM-dropped from no-answer.
- DAILY ATTEMPT CAP AND SAME-DAY DEDUPE OF CONTACT. There is no 'don't call this person again today' rule and no household double-dial guard (the household question is filed as an open question). Calling a husband and wife on the same landline twice in an hour is both a conversion killer and the behavior pattern that generates TCPA harassment complaints. This needs a rule, not a question.
- LANGUAGE PREFERENCE. A meaningful share of US FE leads are Spanish-preferred. In a no-routing silo, a Spanish lead landing on an English-only seller has zero path. The lead field and a manual, admin-logged ownership TRANSFER flow (explicitly not routing) must exist, or the seller quietly lets the lead rot and blames the queue.
- LEAD COST VISIBILITY. Many FE floors charge lead cost back to the agent or set a spend budget. 'This lead cost $32' next to a lead in the queue is the strongest behavioral lever that exists on a floor and it costs one field. At minimum owner/supervisor-visible, and it is the only way lead_source_performance's cost-per-issued ever gets real data.
- NOTHING FEEDS THE LEADERBOARD. The client's fairness mitigation for an all-time revenue board is a secondary recognition category, and this module computes the single best non-tenure-biased candidate on the floor (median speed-to-lead / golden-window hit rate). No event carries it. This is punted to an open question when it should be an emitted metric event the leaderboard module can consume on day one.
- THE CONSENT-UNKNOWN DECISION IS LEFT OPEN AND IT IS THE WHOLE MVP. Imported books are overwhelmingly consent-unknown. If unknown = suppressed, a new client's first login shows an empty queue and 400 suppressed leads — the product looks broken on day one. This needs a designed answer now (e.g. unknown = manual-dial-allowed with a visible 'consent unverified' chip and a one-tap consent capture on the call, but NEVER SMS-able and never dialer-pushable), not a client question.

### Too generic / boilerplate (9)
- THE CORE OBJECT IS BOILERPLATE: a 0-100 weighted score with 8 named factors, model versioning, tier bands, hysteresis + dwell thresholds, sandbox reorder preview and a calibration report is textbook HubSpot/Einstein lead scoring wearing an FE costume. No FE agent has ever said 'this lead is a 73.' What this floor actually needs is a DETERMINISTIC RULE LADDER with named rungs, in this order: (1) promised callback due now, (2) inbound reply/missed call in last 15 min, (3) new ping-post lead inside golden window, (4) underwriting/near-money action outstanding, (5) no-show rebound, (6) appointment prep, (7) aged book by last-touch + attempt cadence. That ladder is explainable without a 'Why this lead?' drawer, has no thrash to suppress, needs no hysteresis, no model_version_id, and no calibration report. Replace the score with the ladder and the module gets simpler AND more credible. Keep a numeric score only as the tiebreaker INSIDE rung 7.
- 'Priority tiers: Call now / Today / This week / Nurture' is generic CRM vocabulary. 'This week' is a fiction in Final Expense — a purchased FE lead that hasn't connected in 72 hours is aged inventory, not a Tuesday task. Rename to the language of the floor: NOW / TODAY / WORKING (the aged grind) / DEAD-ISH (recycle candidate). And the tier a seller sees should carry the RUNG NAME ('promised callback', 'they texted you', 'fresh lead — 3 min'), not a band label.
- 'Best-time-to-call learning: per-lead and per-segment connect-rate histograms by hour-of-week' with scope=lead is B2B SDR boilerplate and is statistically impossible here. An FE lead receives 6-8 attempts in its entire life; a per-lead hour-of-week histogram has 168 buckets and will never cross any confidence floor. Meanwhile the ACTUAL FE answer is already known and doesn't need learning: retirees on landlines answer 9-11am and 4-6:30pm local, and the spec's own insurance_specifics names Social Security deposit timing and then never models it. Ship a hardcoded FE contactability curve as config; delete scope=lead entirely.
- 'Attempt fatigue / diminishing-returns damping' with a damping curve is generic. FE floors don't damp, they run a CADENCE: a defined attempt schedule (e.g. 6 attempts over 14 days, forced across different dayparts, never twice in the same 2-hour window). The rule that matters and is absent: never dial the same lead twice in the same daypart, and cap attempts per lead per day. Express it as a cadence policy, not a score decay.
- 'lead_source_performance.quality_index' is an unfalsifiable composite that no agency owner will trust or act on. The number an FE owner actually buys and fires vendors on is COST PER ISSUED-AND-PAID POLICY and 4th-month persistency by vendor — chargeback-adjusted. A blended 'quality_index' feeding a hidden score weight is exactly the black box this spec claims to reject.
- 'Versioned priority model config with sandbox preview + rollback + change history + side-by-side reorder preview against 200 live leads' is enterprise-config theatre for a tenant that is ONE agency with ONE admin who will touch these weights maybe twice a year. Every hour spent on the diff/preview/rollback UI is an hour not spent on the underwriting chase, which is where the money is.
- The 'Speed-to-Lead & Priority Health (supervisor/admin)' screen is a fifth dashboard duplicating reporting-analytics. This module should EMIT the speed-to-lead facts as events and own zero supervisor charts. The spec's own risks section warns about three modules claiming the ordered list, then builds a supervisor analytics screen that reporting already claims.
- 'Report this ranking is wrong (typified feedback)' is a suggestion box with no consumer. There is no retraining loop in MVP and per-seller weights are explicitly cut. Feedback that changes nothing teaches sellers the system doesn't listen — worse than no button.
- Four separate anti-thrash mechanisms (band hysteresis, dwell threshold, tier-lock during active call, session order freeze) to solve one problem. This is generic over-engineering. Keep the session freeze — a seller's list is frozen from 'start calling' to 'stop calling', period — and delete the other three.

### Bloat to cut (10)
- priority_score_history as an append-only row per recompute. With ~50 sellers x ~300 leads and minute-level decay recompute, this is millions of rows a month against a <$100/month infra budget, to serve a post-MVP calibration report and a 'why was it ranked that way at 2:14 PM' question no one on a commission floor will ever ask. CUT to: write a history row ONLY on tier change or escalation, retention 30 days.
- The 0-100 score, factor_breakdown_json, model_version_id and the entire 'Why this lead?' drawer. Nothing breaks if you delete them — because with the rule ladder proposed above, the reason IS the rank ('promised callback, 2:00 PM'). The drawer only exists to explain an opacity the score itself created. Cut the score, cut the drawer, keep primary_reason_text.
- Priority Model Settings screen: sandbox preview against live data, activation, rollback, version history, diff_summary. CUT to a single settings form with ~8 numbers (golden window per source, cold days, attempt cadence, daypart rules, tier thresholds) and an audit row. Nothing breaks. 'Preview how today's queues would reorder' is a demo feature for a buyer who will never use it twice.
- best_time_profile with scope=lead. Statistically impossible sample size, and it invites a confidence-floor mechanism whose only job is to disable itself. Cut scope=lead; keep at most a hardcoded FE/IUL daypart config.
- lead_source_performance owned by THIS module. This is a reporting entity with a reporting audience. Owning it here violates the module's own stated boundary discipline and drags cost-per-issued (which has no data source in MVP) into the scoring path. Reporting owns it; this module reads a single per-source multiplier if it wants one.
- The 'Speed-to-Lead & Priority Health' supervisor screen. Emit the events; let reporting-analytics render. Building it here is the second dashboard that competes with reporting the same way the spec warns tasks/kanban compete over the ordered list.
- 'Report this ranking is wrong' feedback capture — no consumer, no loop, no MVP value.
- Score calibration report as a designed feature. With the rule ladder there is nothing to calibrate; with a score, calibration needs months of closed-won volume that won't exist at launch. What survives is one honest number in reporting: contact rate and close rate for calls made on the top-10 vs off-queue.
- Tier hysteresis + dwell thresholds + tier-lock-during-active-call. Three mechanisms for one problem. Keep 'freeze the order for the duration of a calling session' and delete the rest.
- Four tiers. Three is enough. 'This week' will become the drawer everything gets pushed into, and then nobody looks at it — which is a nurture bucket with a misleading name.

### Event gaps (12)
- PING-POST IS A TWO-STEP PROTOCOL AND THE EVENT MODEL DOESN'T KNOW IT. There is no lead.ping_received / lead.post_accepted, and — critically — no vendor_generated_at (the timestamp the consumer actually submitted the form) anywhere in a payload. The golden window is started from OUR lead.created. Vendors post seconds to minutes after generation, and resold/aged leads post hours or days after generation. Starting the clock at ingest means an aged resold lead enters the queue at maximum freshness and outranks a genuinely 4-minute-old lead. This silently falsifies the module's north-star KPI and its #1 differentiator. Every intake payload needs vendor_generated_at + posted_at + our received_at, and the decay must run off the vendor timestamp with a sanity clamp.
- NO EVENT FOR THE FIRST ATTEMPT. lead.first_attempt_made (or speed_to_lead.recorded) does not exist, yet time-to-first-attempt is declared the module's north star and is consumed by reporting, supervisor oversight and (should be) the leaderboard. As specced, other modules must read lead_attempt_stats out of this module's tables — exactly the cross-module DB coupling this spec claims to avoid. Emit the success event, not only the failure event (lead.freshness_expired).
- THE ALOAI SUMMARY ARRIVES LATER THAN THE CALL. The spec reacts to call.completed with 'scan the AloAi summary for a callback promise suggestion.' Aloware delivers recording, transcription and AI summary as SEPARATE, asynchronous webhooks minutes after the call ends. At call.completed the summary does not exist. Needs call.recording_ready / call.transcription_ready / call.summary_ready as distinct consumed events, with the promise-suggestion path bound to the summary event.
- lead.went_cold OWNERSHIP IS AN UNRESOLVED DEFECT, NOT AN OPEN QUESTION. This module owns days_since_last_touch, the T-24h warning and the recycle heuristics — it owns the clock. Filing 'who emits cold?' as a question guarantees either a double-emitted cold flag or two disagreeing clocks. Rule it now: whoever owns the recency clock owns the cold transition. If pipeline keeps the flag, this module must stop owning days_since_last_touch and read it.
- SUPPRESSION CONFLATES LEGAL BLOCKS WITH BUSINESS DEFERRALS. meeting.scheduled 'suppresses from the call queue'; snooze also leaves the queue; DNC also suppresses — all funneling into is_suppressed / lead.contact_suppressed and into one 'Snoozed & Suppressed' screen. That pollutes the compliance evidence trail that the spec itself lists as a TCPA/CCPA artifact and makes the 'compliance integrity = zero' KPI unmeasurable. Split hard: contact_blocked (legal: consent/DNC/opt-out/calling window, immutable, auditable, no override) vs work_deferred (business: snoozed, meeting booked, sequence running, cold). Different events, different tables, different screens.
- ENTITY CONFUSION ACROSS EVERY EVENT: lead_id, opportunity_id and contact are used interchangeably. Phase 1 pattern #1 explicitly decouples Opportunity from Contact, yet lead_priority_score is keyed per (opportunity, owner) while suppression, attempt stats, contactability and half the events are keyed on lead_id. Unanswered and consequential: a contact with an FE opportunity AND an IUL opportunity appears twice in the queue with the same phone number and one consent state — and gets double-dialed. Pick the queue's unit (it should be CONTACT for dialing/compliance, with the top-ranked opportunity as the reason) and rewrite the payloads.
- PII IN THE EVENT BUS. lead.priority_escalated carries contact_phone_e164 and tcpa_consent_status to notifications, automations, mobile and dialer. Consent can change between emission and dial; a cached consent flag riding an event is exactly how a non-compliant dial happens, and it spreads phone numbers to consumers that don't need them. Events carry ids; the gate is re-evaluated at the dial action, server-side.
- NO DIALER SESSION LIFECYCLE. The spec freezes queue order 'until the session ends' and pushes top-N to a Power Dialer list, but consumes no dialer.session_started / dialer.session_ended / dialer.list_exhausted, and emits priority.queue_built with no matching queue_consumed/abandoned. Result: the 'queue adherence' KPI has no data path and the frozen order has no unfreeze trigger.
- NO CARRIER-LEVEL FAILURE SIGNALS. Only human dispositions are consumed. Aged FE lists are full of disconnected landlines; sms.delivery_failed, call.failed_invalid_number and carrier SIP failure codes are the highest-volume 'bad number' signal and are more reliable than a rushed agent disposition. Missing entirely.
- MISSING COUNTERPART EVENTS: lead.unsnoozed, override.expired, promise.canceled, pin.expired. Kanban badges and My Day rows will go stale the moment a snooze or pin expires by time rather than by user action, because nothing fires on expiry.
- NO priority.recompute_completed. priority.model_activated triggers a tenant-wide recompute and a 'your queue order was updated' notification — sellers get told the queue changed while it is half-recomputed. Notify on completion, not on activation.
- NO EVENT PATH FOR THE MONEY LAYER named in missing_for_real_operation: nothing consumed for policy.issued, application.signature_pending, requirement.outstanding, draft.failed, policy.lapse_pending. The 'near-money first' factor has no wire into it.

### 🚨 Silo violations (6)
- THE ALOWARE SIDE OF THE SILO IS UNDEFENDED — the most serious one. Aloware has its OWN contact ownership and visibility model. Pushing 'the top N for the owning seller' into an Aloware Power Dialer list, and pushing contacts via the Lead/Form push API, can land those contacts in a shared Aloware workspace where any Aloware user can see, search and dial them. Our CRM silo is only as strong as the weakest system in the stack, and the spec never states the required Aloware-side model (per-agent lists, per-agent contact ownership, no shared queues, what happens on contact collision when two sellers legitimately own different people with the same number). This must be a designed constraint with a verification step, not an assumption.
- lead.assigned_owner_changed IS CONSUMED, WHICH MEANS OWNERSHIP TRANSFER EXISTS SOMEWHERE — and no module names it, gates it, or logs it. That is routing sneaking in the back door. The spec must state: transfer is manual, admin-only, one lead at a time, fully audit-logged, and never bulk or rule-based. Otherwise the first feature request after launch ('can you move Maria's book to Carlos?') becomes an assignment engine.
- CROSS-SELLER OUTCOMES SILENTLY REORDER ANOTHER SELLER'S QUEUE. lead_source_performance is tenant-level and feeds the base priority of new leads; best_time_profile scopes to source/age_band/timezone across the tenant. That's other sellers' results changing my ordering. Probably acceptable, but it is undeclared cross-seller coupling and it needs (a) an explicit statement, (b) a minimum-N floor so a seller can't infer a colleague's performance from a small-sample vendor stat, and (c) removal of any drill-through from an aggregate to lead-level data for a seller.
- SUPERVISOR READ-ONLY QUEUE VIEW SHOWS OTHER SELLERS' LEAD NAMES, PREMIUMS AND SUPPRESSION REASONS with no access logging. Cross-seller visibility is legitimate for supervisor/admin, but this is the widest PII surface in the module and the spec only logs EXPORTS. Under CCPA, every supervisor view of another seller's book should be access-logged, not just exports.
- THE 'Speed-to-Lead & Priority Health' seller fallback is correct in text ('you see your own only') but the module also computes queue adherence and override pressure PER SELLER. Those are performance metrics on a floor with a public leaderboard — the boundary between 'private per-seller ops metric' and 'thing that leaks into standings' must be stated explicitly, or someone will helpfully surface 'queue adherence' on the leaderboard and turn a coaching metric into a public shaming metric.
- NOT A VIOLATION BUT THE SILO'S UNPRICED COST, and the spec looks away: with no routing, a fresh ping-post lead owned by an unavailable seller simply dies. The module correctly refuses to reassign and then treats the consequence as a reporting line item. The silo decision is the client's, but this module is where its cost becomes visible, and the spec owes an explicit designed mitigation (owner-side escalation, vendor ping rejection when the owner is offline, supervisor alert without write access) rather than an open question.

## Risks & dependencies
- **Hard dependency on Aloware webhooks for truth.** Recency, attempt counts, dispositions, transcripts and AI summaries all arrive as webhooks. If they are delayed or dropped, the queue silently ranks on stale data and sellers re-call people they just spoke to. Mitigation: idempotent ingestion keyed on the provider call id, a reconciliation sweep against the Contact Lookup/call APIs, and a visible 'last synced' indicator on the queue rather than pretending freshness.
- **Time-zone derivation is legally consequential and imperfect.** NPA-to-time-zone is wrong for ported mobiles and for several split-zone states. Mitigation: prefer explicit lead-provided state/ZIP, record timezone_source on every suppression decision, and default to the MOST restrictive window when sources conflict. A wrong window call is a statutory exposure, not a UX bug.
- **We must mirror, never duplicate, Aloware's DNC and opt-out state.** Two competing suppression lists that disagree is worse than one imperfect list. Dependency on comms-messaging to relay STOP/opt-out and DNC events reliably.
- **Queue thrash / score churn.** Event-driven recompute on a busy floor can reorder a seller's list every few seconds. Mitigated by tier hysteresis, dwell thresholds, session-level order freeze and only emitting lead.priority_changed on band crossings — but this must be tested at ~50 sellers before it is believed.
- **Infra budget (<$100/month) vs real-time recompute.** A naive cron scanning every lead every minute will blow the budget and the database. Design constraint: incremental, event-triggered recompute plus a narrow scheduled sweep for time-only factors (decay, window opening, stale countdown) driven by next_recompute_at indexes, not full table scans.
- **Ownership overlap with tasks/My Day and pipeline-kanban.** Three modules can each plausibly claim 'the ordered list of what to do'. This module must own ORDERING and REASON only; tasks owns the activity objects and reminders; kanban owns the board. If that boundary blurs, we ship two competing to-do lists — the exact 'simple to use, brutal to administer' anti-pattern.
- **Dependency on lead-intake for ping-post ingestion and consent certificates.** Without a real ping-post endpoint capturing created_at at second resolution and storing the certificate, the freshness factor and the compliance gate are both fiction.
- **Over-trusting AI call summaries.** AloAi-extracted callback times will sometimes be wrong ('call me next week' parsed as a specific slot). Never auto-commit: suggestions require one-tap confirmation, and confidence is stored.
- **Model credibility risk.** If tier A does not out-convert tier C in the calibration report, sellers will (correctly) abandon the queue. The calibration report is not a nice-to-have; it is the module's own kill switch.
- **CUT — cross-seller lead recommendation ('this lead would convert better with Maria').** That is routing, which is explicitly out of scope and would breach the silo model.
- **CUT — email open/click as a scoring signal.** Apple Mail Privacy Protection and bot prefetch make opens near-random, and treating a machine open as buying intent would put dead leads at the top of the queue.
- **CUT — per-seller editable scoring weights.** It makes 50 incomparable models, destroys the calibration report and turns support into archaeology. Sellers get filters, pins, snoozes and skip reasons instead.
- **CUT — a standalone seller-facing 'lead score dashboard' and an A-F letter grade.** Redundant with the queue and the kanban badge; a second vocabulary for the same concept guarantees confusion.
- **CUT — auto-recycling of cold/lost leads.** The client decided recycling is a manual action in MVP; automated re-contact of a lost lead is also the fastest way to generate a TCPA complaint. We suggest, a human decides.

## Open questions
- Who emits lead.went_cold — this module (which owns the recency clock and the stale countdown) or lead-management/pipeline? This spec assumes we CONSUME it and only emit lead.stale_warning_raised. One owner must be picked in consolidation, or the cold flag will be double-emitted.
- Does 'My Day' live in tasks-activities (rendering our ordering) or here? Recommendation: tasks renders and owns the activity objects; we supply the order and the reason string. Needs an explicit ruling so we do not ship two lists.
- Real ping-post vendor list, payload shape and monthly volume are still open (OQ-2). The golden-window defaults (5 min / 60 min) are industry-derived placeholders until we see actual vendor behavior.
- Is the Aloware Power Dialer list push in MVP or V1.1? It is the difference between 'a better list' and a measurable attempts-per-hour lift, but it is the highest-complexity integration in this module.
- Should FE and IUL be separate pipelines (and therefore separate queues with separate profiles), or one pipeline with a product field? This decision belongs to pipeline-kanban but directly determines whether product-track profiles are configuration or a hard split.
- Time-zone source of truth: do purchased lead payloads reliably include state/ZIP, or must we derive from NPA? This changes the legal risk profile of the calling-window gate.
- Can supervisors snooze or pin inside a seller's queue? This spec says NO (it is assignment by another name and breaks the silo model). Confirm with the client — an owner may expect coaching intervention.
- How are household/spousal leads (very common in FE) modeled — two leads, one lead with two contacts, or a linked pair? It affects dedupe, attempt counting and double-dialing the same household.
- Should the score contribute anything to the public leaderboard (e.g. a 'speed-to-lead' or 'Most Improved' secondary category, which research recommends as the fairness mitigation for an all-time revenue board)? Median speed-to-lead is an excellent, non-tenure-biased secondary category — but the leaderboard module owns that call.
- Retention window for priority_score_history: long enough to backtest weight changes, short enough to respect the infra budget and CCPA minimization. Proposed 180 days, unconfirmed.
- When consent status is unknown (not denied, just missing), does the lead appear in the queue as call-only, or is it fully suppressed? Legally the safe answer is suppressed; commercially that may hide a large share of an imported book. Needs a client decision with the risk stated plainly.

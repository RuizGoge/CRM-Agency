# 5. Tasks & Follow-up (Activity Engine, Cadences & Speed-to-Lead SLA)

> **Module id:** `tasks` · part of [`docs/02-functional-map.md`](../02-functional-map.md)

## Verdict
KEEP — but RESHAPE from "a to-do list module" into **the Activity Engine of the whole system**, and rename it internally to that. Three reshapes: (1) It does NOT own a separate "task" object next to calls/SMS/meetings — it owns ONE `activity` record that every other module writes into (Comms logs calls/SMS into it, Calendar links meetings to it, Pipeline stage moves spawn it). If Tasks owns a private to-do list while calls live elsewhere, "My Day" becomes a lie and the seller goes back to a notepad. (2) It does NOT own the per-lead timeline UI (Lead 360 renders it) nor the meeting record (Calendar owns it) — it owns the *planned* side (what is due, why, when, and whether it slipped) and the *sequencing* side. (3) It absorbs the **Speed-to-Lead SLA clock**, which was floating between lead-intake and dialer: the SLA is meaningless unless it lands as the #1 row of My Day with a countdown. Do NOT merge into Pipeline — the kanban answers "where is this deal", My Day answers "what do I do in the next 4 minutes"; those are different questions and merging them produces the exact "board as a to-do list" failure that makes sellers stop updating stages. In a silo model with no routing engine, this module is the only thing standing between a purchased FE lead and silence — it is the second most load-bearing module after Pipeline.

## Purpose & value
It tells the seller exactly who to contact right now and why — a single urgency-ranked list of every call due, reply waiting, appointment prep, and post-quote follow-up on their own book — and it automatically keeps chasing (call + SMS + email) until the lead answers or books, so no purchased FE lead dies in the first 5 minutes and no quoted IUL prospect is forgotten on day 3.

## Screens & states (12)

### My Day (seller home)
Answer 'who do I contact right now' in under three seconds and let the seller act without leaving the screen.

**Key actions:** `Call / Text / Email in one tap (opens Aloware click-to-call or comms composer)` · `Log outcome from the row` · `Snooze with presets` · `Mark done` · `Open lead 360` · `Add ad-hoc follow-up` · `Start Focus Mode` · `Filter by type (calls / replies / appointments / cadence steps)`

- **Empty:** Headline: 'Your day is clear.' Body: 'Nothing is due right now — and nothing is overdue. Keep it that way: 3 of your open leads have no next step scheduled.' Primary button: 'Schedule follow-ups (3)'. Secondary: 'Add a follow-up'. For a brand-new seller with zero leads: Headline: 'No leads yet.' Body: 'When a lead lands in your book, it shows up here with a 60-second call clock. Add your first lead to try it.' Primary: 'Add a lead'.
- **Loading:** Skeleton rows in the Now/Overdue/Later groups, group headers rendered immediately with counts from cache; the SLA countdown row renders first and independently so a hot lead is never delayed by the rest of the list.
- **Error:** Inline banner: 'We couldn't load your day. Your leads and follow-ups are safe.' with 'Try again'. If only the Aloware call status is unavailable: 'Calling is temporarily unavailable — you can still text, email, and log outcomes.' Any outcome logged while offline is queued and re-sent with a 'Saved, syncing' chip.
- **No permission:** Not reachable for supervisor/admin as a personal feed — they land on Team Follow-up Health instead. If a supervisor deep-links to a seller's My Day: 'This is a seller's personal work queue. You can see their follow-up health and activity metrics, but not act on their leads.' with a link to the seller's health card.
- **Mobile:** Single column; hot-lead SLA banner sticky at top with a full-width 'Call now' button; swipe right = done, swipe left = snooze; long-press = outcome sheet; bottom action bar for Call/Text/Note; completions queue offline.

### Focus Mode (run the queue)
Agent-paced execution of the current queue, one lead at a time, with zero navigation between calls.

**Key actions:** `Dial` · `Log outcome and auto-advance` · `Skip / snooze this one` · `Send a templated SMS` · `Book appointment` · `Exit and return to My Day` · `Push queue to Aloware Power Dialer`

- **Empty:** Headline: 'Nothing left in the queue.' Body: 'You worked every call that was due. Want to pull the next 20 leads with no scheduled next step?' Primary: 'Build a queue'. Secondary: 'Back to My Day'.
- **Loading:** Card frame with lead name and phone shown as soon as the first record resolves; script/notes and history panes fill in progressively; the dial button is disabled with 'Preparing…' until consent and DNC status are confirmed.
- **Error:** 'We couldn't reach the dialer. The queue is saved — you can keep logging outcomes manually or retry.' with 'Retry' and 'Log manually'. If consent/DNC status cannot be verified: dialing stays blocked with 'We can't confirm contact permission for this number right now. Skipped for safety.'
- **No permission:** Seller-only. Supervisor/admin see 'Focus Mode runs on a seller's own book, which is private to them.'
- **Mobile:** Full-screen card, giant call button, outcome buttons as a fixed bottom grid; keeps screen awake during a call.

## Typical automations
- When a purchased lead is created -> start the 60-second call clock, pin a 'Call now' row at the top of the owner's My Day, push a hot-lead notification, and enroll in the FE Fresh Lead Day-1 Blitz.
- When the call clock passes 50% with no touch -> send an urgent notification to the owning seller only (never to another seller — silos hold even for hot leads).
- When the call clock expires with no touch -> mark the SLA breached, log it to the seller's and supervisor's follow-up health, and keep the row pinned until worked.
- When a lead replies by SMS, emails back, or calls in -> pause every active cadence, cancel pending automated texts, and create a top-priority 'Reply to {first_name}' activity due now with a response clock.
- When an appointment is booked -> pause the prospecting cadence, create a prep task before the meeting and a confirmation touch the day before, in the lead's local time.
- When an appointment is no-showed -> create a callback activity due immediately and enroll the lead in the No-Show Recovery cadence.
- When a deal enters the Quoted/Presented stage -> enroll the product-appropriate post-quote cadence (fast clock for Final Expense, long clock for IUL illustrations).
- When a deal leaves the Quoted stage or is closed -> exit the post-quote cadence so the prospect never gets a 'did you get my quote?' text after signing.
- When a deal is Closed-Won -> exit all prospecting cadences and enroll the post-sale lifecycle: policy delivery confirmation, day-before first-draft-date call, free-look check-in, and persistency touches.
- When a deal is Closed-Lost -> exit all cadences, cancel pending touches, and schedule one 'Review for recycling' activity at the configured horizon.
- When a call ends with 'no answer' or 'voicemail' -> auto-complete the planned call from the Aloware webhook, increment the attempt counter, and schedule the next attempt at the cadence's next interval inside allowed hours.
- When a call ends with 'connected' -> auto-complete the activity, attach recording, transcript and AI summary to the lead timeline, and prompt the seller for a next step before the drawer closes.
- When a contact texts STOP -> immediately cancel every pending SMS step for that lead, flag consent as revoked, and tell the seller in plain English why the cadence stopped.
- When a step would fire outside 8:00am-9:00pm in the lead's local time -> hold it until the next allowed window instead of sending or dropping it.
- When an SMS step has no valid consent on file -> skip that step, log the reason, and continue the cadence with call and email touches only.
- When an activity is more than 24 hours overdue -> move it into Overdue Triage and notify the owning seller; roll it into the supervisor's health metrics without exposing the lead.
- When a lead has had no activity for 7 days (configurable) -> mark it cold and create a revive activity on the owner's My Day.
- When an open lead has no scheduled next activity -> surface it in the 'no next step' counter and offer one-click cadence enrollment.
- When the attempt ceiling for a lead is reached -> stop the cadence and create a single decision activity: recycle, mark cold, or close as lost.
- When a seller is deactivated -> freeze their pending automated sends and prompt an admin to reassign the book so the follow-ups do not go dark.
- Every morning at the seller's chosen time -> send a digest of today's plan: hot leads, appointments, overdue count, and the number of leads with no next step.

## KPIs
- Median time to first touch on purchased leads (target: under the configured SLA, default 60s) — the headline speed-to-lead number
- SLA compliance rate: % of first_contact clocks met, per seller and per lead vendor
- Inbound response time: median minutes to reply to an inbound SMS or missed call
- Open leads with NO scheduled next activity (count and % of book) — the leak metric; target zero
- Overdue activity backlog per seller and age of the oldest overdue item
- Same-day completion rate: % of activities completed on their due date
- Attempts to first contact: average dial/SMS/email attempts before a connected outcome, by lead vendor
- Contact rate per cadence and per step (which touch actually connects)
- Auto-pause rate: % of enrollments paused by a lead reply or booking — the proxy for cadence relevance
- Post-quote cadence conversion: % of opportunities entering Quoted that reach Closed-Won while enrolled vs not enrolled
- No-show recovery rate: % of no-showed appointments re-booked within 72h
- Snooze pressure: average snoozes per activity and count of 3+ snooze (avoided) leads
- Auto-logging coverage: % of calls completed via Aloware webhook rather than manual entry (target >95%) — the data-rot indicator
- Quiet-hours holds and consent skips per week (compliance health, should be non-zero and explainable)
- Opt-out rate per cadence (early warning that a template is too aggressive)
- Adoption: % of active sellers who complete at least one activity from My Day daily

## Insurance specifics (Life / FE / IUL) & vertical-agnostic core
Genuinely Life/FE/IUL-specific here — and all of it lives in CONFIG + SEEDED TEMPLATES, never in the engine: (1) **Ping-post decay.** Purchased FE/final-expense leads are sold to multiple agents and decay in minutes, so the SLA target is expressed in SECONDS (default 60s to first dial attempt), not the "same business day" that a B2B CRM assumes; the SLA row physically outranks everything else in My Day and can pin a "Call now" affordance above the fold. (2) **Day-1 blitz shape.** FE contact rates require ~6-8 dial attempts concentrated in the first 24-48h with spaced retries, not one call a day for a week — the seeded template encodes that curve. (3) **Senior demographic call windows.** FE prospects are 50-85; mid-morning and early-afternoon local windows outperform evenings, so cadence steps carry preferred-window hints per template. (4) **Post-quote is where FE/IUL deals actually die** — FE between quote and application/voice-signature, IUL between illustration delivery and decision (weeks, not days) — so a stage-bound cadence auto-enrolls on entry to the Quoted/Presented stage, with different clocks for FE (hours/days) vs IUL (days/weeks). (5) **Underwriting-requirement chasing**: activity subtypes for voice signature, phone health interview, APS/medical records, missing bank draft info — these are follow-ups on a deal that is already "sold" but not issued. (6) **Persistency & the money-after-the-sale problem**: first-premium **draft date** reminders (call the day before to prevent an NSF that lapses the policy), policy-delivery follow-up, free-look-period check-in, and early-month persistency touches — because a lapsed FE policy is a clawed-back sale even though Commission itself is post-MVP. (7) **TCPA is a scheduling constraint, not a checkbox**: no activity may be auto-executed outside 8:00am-9:00pm in the LEAD's local time zone, SMS steps refuse to run without a stored consent flag/vendor consent certificate, and a STOP instantly cancels every pending SMS step. **How the core stays vertical-agnostic:** the engine only knows `activity`, `sequence`, `sequence_step`, `sla_clock`, `quiet_hours_policy`, and "enroll when stage X is entered". Product type (FE/IUL/other), attempt curves, window hints, activity subtypes, SLA targets per lead source, and the template library are all tenant-level configuration and seed data. Adding auto/health/P&C means shipping a new template pack and new subtypes — zero engine changes.

## ⚔️ Adversarial review (sales-ops critic)

**Verdict:** NEEDS WORK — the reshape into an Activity Engine is right and the compliance-as-scheduler stance is the best thing in the document, but the flagship speed-to-lead feature is wired to the wrong events (stops on call.completed, no outbound send events, no dial-initiated signal) and has no answer for a seller who is asleep, off-shift or on a call, so it will breach on day one; on top of that it invents a supervisor tier that contradicts the validated model, smuggles in supervisor writes on another seller's leads, and carries roughly a third of its surface as generic task-manager and enterprise-sequencing bloat.

### Must fix — missing for real operation (13)
- SELLER AVAILABILITY vs the 60-second clock — the module's single biggest hole. With no routing engine, a ping-post lead that lands at 11:40pm, on a Sunday, or while the seller is on a live 25-minute FE presentation, starts a 60s clock that is guaranteed to breach. The spec has a `paused_for_quiet_hours` field but never defines the behavior, and there is no seller shift/availability window, no 'on a call' suppression, and no vendor delivery-window setting. As written, day one produces a wall of red breaches, the metric becomes noise, and the SLA dashboard is dead within a week. Need: seller working-hours config, SLA clock states (running / held-off-shift / held-on-call), and an explicit rule that off-shift arrivals start the clock at shift open, plus a vendor-side 'do not deliver outside these hours' setting to push back on the lead vendor.
- The SLA stop condition is wrong in a way that breaks the headline feature. The clock 'stops on the first outbound call/SMS/email logged' and the only call event consumed is `call.completed`. A seller who dials at 0:22 and talks for 18 minutes shows BREACHED. The clock must stop on dial initiation (call.initiated / dial_attempted, stamped client-side at click-to-call time and reconciled by webhook), not on call completion.
- Nothing prevents SLA gaming. Because any outbound SMS stops a first_contact clock, an auto-send SMS step at second 5 makes every lead 'met' while nobody dials. For sla_class=first_contact the stop event must be a DIAL, full stop; SMS/email may satisfy inbound_response only.
- Voicemail drop / pre-recorded VM is absent. It is standard equipment on a US FE floor (it is how attempts 2-6 stay affordable) and it is also a TCPA-sensitive artifact (ringless voicemail litigation). Either specify it as a step channel with its own consent/quiet-hours treatment or state explicitly that it is out of scope and why.
- No scheduled-callback object distinct from a Calendar meeting. FE telesales runs on 'call me back Thursday at 2, my wife is home then'. That is a hard-time commitment but it is not an appointment and the spec routes everything time-bound through Calendar. Need activity_subtype=scheduled_callback with a hard due time, its own reminder, and its own no-show equivalent.
- No income/deposit-date field driving the money conversation. FE prospects are on Social Security or a pension; affordability, draft date selection and the best time to close are all anchored to the SSA payment cycle (Wed schedule by birth date) or the 1st/3rd. The spec has a day-before draft reminder but no source field for it and no concept of 'this prospect can only be closed after the 3rd'.
- Underwriting-chase subtypes (voice signature, APS, health interview, missing bank draft info) have no policy/application context to point at — no carrier, no application/policy number, no e-app link, no requirement due date from the carrier. 'Chase APS' with no case number is an empty row a seller will delete. Either declare a hard dependency on a policy/application record owned by Pipeline, or own the minimal fields here; right now it is neither.
- Lead-vendor credit/return window is missing entirely. Agencies get money back for wrong-number/deceased/never-inquired leads if disputed inside the vendor's 24-72h window. The disposition should raise a vendor-credit flag with a countdown and a per-vendor return report. This is direct cash recovery and it is exactly the kind of thing an owner tests a CRM on.
- Lead language is not modeled. A large share of US FE books are Spanish-speaking, and this determines which SMS template a cadence step must send TODAY — not after the i18n phase. Cadence steps need a per-language template variant and a lead.language attribute contract.
- Recording handling ignores state law. Activities store recording URLs and AI transcripts, and the seller-side announcement is Aloware's problem, but two-party-consent states (CA, FL, PA, WA, IL and others) plus CCPA retention mean this module must record which state's rule applied and support retention/anonymization on the recording pointer. The audit entity is there; the policy is not.
- Concurrency is undefined: two ping-post leads landing 10 seconds apart both pin as 'Call now'. There is no rule for which one wins, no 'you have 3 hot leads' affordance, and no back-pressure to stop a vendor feed the seller cannot absorb.
- The leaderboard contract is hand-waved. `leaderboard-secondary-metrics` appears as a consumer three times but the module never declares WHICH counters it publishes, at what latency, or how they reset. Since the client's fairness mitigation (a secondary recognition category on an all-time board) depends on activity data, this module must name the exact metrics it owns (e.g. dials, contacts, SLA compliance %, attempts-to-contact) and confirm they are real-time like Earnings.
- Offline mobile completion queue and Aloware webhook auto-completion can both complete the same activity. No conflict/idempotency rule is stated for that collision (only for the Aloware call id), so a seller who logs 'no answer' offline can be overwritten by, or duplicate, the webhook's disposition.

### Too generic / boilerplate (6)
- Priority ranking is described as 'a deterministic priority score' with an ordered list of buckets, but never states the actual weighting. In an FE/IUL book the tiebreaker is money: an IUL at $12,000 annual premium sitting post-illustration outranks a $540 FE callback, always. Spec must publish the literal formula (sla_class weight, then stage weight, then deal_value/annual-premium band, then age-of-last-touch) and pin it in config — otherwise every seller reads the list as arbitrary and reverts to their own order.
- The outcome taxonomy ('connected / no answer / voicemail left / wrong number / not interested / callback requested / appointment set / quote presented / application started') is stock B2B sales-engagement. A real FE floor disposition list needs: spouse/other decision-maker not present, already covered (name the competing carrier), can't afford after budget question, health-knockout (specify condition), bank-draft objection, hung up / hostile, wrong number - VENDOR CREDIT, language = Spanish, and callback tied to income deposit date. Two of those (vendor credit, health knockout) are money and compliance, not analytics garnish.
- Focus Mode is written as a generic 'run the queue' screen. It never resolves the verified Aloware constraint: we have NO embeddable softphone. The only real dial paths are the Talk Chrome extension (desktop only, extension must be installed and detected) and the Two-Legged Call API (rings the SELLER's phone first, then the lead — 3-8 seconds of latency and a seller-side answer step). The whole 'one-tap Call' promise across My Day, Focus Mode and Mobile is unbuildable as written and must be re-specified per path, including a browser-extension-not-detected state.
- 'Snooze with presets and anti-avoidance rules', 'Overdue triage & bulk reschedule', 'Duplicate-activity suppression', 'Command palette actions', 'Audit trail', 'Notification preferences' are copy-paste task-manager features. Each is defensible individually, but none is shaped by FE reality (e.g. snooze presets should be 'after the 3rd' and 'after their SSA deposit', not 'next Monday'; overdue triage should sort by annual premium band and vendor credit window, not just age).
- Cadence analytics is generic sequence reporting (connect/reply/opt-out/appointment rate). The only two numbers an FE owner actually manages by are missing: contact rate BY ATTEMPT NUMBER (proves whether attempts 4-8 earn their cost) and cost-per-contact BY LEAD VENDOR (proves which ping-post feed to cut). Without vendor_id in the analytics grain this module cannot answer the question that funds it.
- The seller Speed-to-Lead dashboard shows 'an anonymized team median for context'. That is generic CRM hedging and it contradicts this product's own decision: performance is PUBLIC here (leaderboard is transparent to everyone). Either speed-to-lead compliance is a public, named, ranked metric surfaced through the leaderboard, or it is private to the seller and their supervisor. Anonymized medians are the worst of both.

### Bloat to cut (10)
- Supervisor-created 'coaching activity' on a seller's lead (source=supervisor_coaching) plus 'Flag a seller for coaching (creates a coaching note)'. Nobody asked for it, it invents a whole write-permission surface into another seller's book, and it is the seed of the routing/assignment engine we decided not to build. Cut both from MVP; coaching happens on the health screen or in person.
- Sequence versioning with version pinning on running enrollments (`sequence_version`, `cloned_from_sequence_id`, `version`). This is enterprise sequence-tooling complexity for a 50-seat agency with ~7 templates. MVP rule: edits apply to steps not yet materialized, with a warning banner showing how many live enrollments are affected. Saves a genuinely hard migration path.
- `branch_on_outcome` on sequence_step. Branching cadences are the classic complexity sink — they triple the state machine, the preview, and the QA surface, and templates that branch are the ones sellers stop trusting. MVP: linear steps + pause/exit rules only.
- Two separate parked-work surfaces: the 'Snoozed & Waiting view' feature and the 'Enrollments & Waiting' screen describe the same thing. One screen.
- The whole seller-facing Speed-to-Lead dashboard screen. A live countdown in My Day plus a three-tile strip (median first touch, compliance %, clocks running) covers it; a dedicated historical dashboard for a seller who owns 200 leads is a screen they open twice. Fold into follow-up health.
- 'Duplicate-activity suppression' as an engine feature that merges rows and combines reason chips. Over-built. Enforce a uniqueness rule at creation (one open activity per lead+channel+day, later request loses and appends its reason) and ship the merge UI never.
- `follow_up_health_snapshot` with 12 pre-computed metrics before anyone has used the supervisor screen. Ship 4 (leads_without_next_activity, overdue_count, median_first_touch_seconds, sla_compliance_pct) and add the rest when a supervisor asks.
- `holiday_calendar_ref` in quiet_hours_policy. YAGNI at MVP and a maintenance liability. Keep blocked_weekdays (some states restrict Sunday calling — that one is real), drop the holiday calendar.
- Admin 'read-only support mode' to view any seller's My Day. Admin already has global visibility through the health screen and the audit log; a per-seller impersonation view is an undefined, high-risk surface built for a support scenario that does not exist yet in a 50-person single-tenant deployment.
- The full cadence-performance funnel with 'downstream closed-won contribution' attribution. Attribution across a 60-day IUL cycle is a research project. Ship connect-rate-by-attempt-number and opt-out rate; both are cheap and both change behavior.

### Event gaps (14)
- `call.initiated` (or dial_attempted) is neither emitted nor consumed. The entire speed-to-lead feature depends on it: consuming only `call.completed` means the clock runs for the duration of the call and breaches on every long first conversation. This is the module's most serious wiring defect.
- No outbound comms events are consumed at all. events_consumed has sms.received, sms.opt_out, sms.delivery_failed, email.received — but no `sms.sent`, `email.sent`, `sms.delivered`. So auto_send steps have no success signal, the activity that represents them can never be truthfully completed, and the claim that an outbound SMS/email stops the SLA clock has nothing to listen to.
- Command/event conflation: `sequence.step_executed` is described as 'either an auto-send goes out or an activity is materialized'. Sending is a COMMAND to Comms (comms.send_requested) and step_executed is a FACT that should only be emitted after Comms confirms. As specified, a step is 'executed' even when the SMS never left, and the seller sees a green step for a message the prospect never got.
- `status` enum on activity is planned|snoozed|in_progress|completed|cancelled|expired, but the sms.delivery_failed reaction says 'mark the step's activity as failed'. There is no failed state. Data model and reaction contradict each other.
- No `sla.paused` / `sla.resumed` events despite the `paused_for_quiet_hours` field existing on sla_clock. If clocks can be held (and they must be, see off-shift handling), the hold has to be an auditable event or SLA compliance % is unreconcilable and unarguable in a coaching conversation.
- Cold-lead ownership is circular. This module is the source of truth for last-touch (it owns activity), yet it CONSUMES `lead.went_cold` computed elsewhere. Either Tasks emits `lead.no_activity_threshold_reached` and Lead Management reacts, or Tasks exposes last_touch_at as the canonical read. As written, two modules compute 'days since last touch' independently and they will diverge — and that number is printed on the kanban card.
- `sequence.enrolled` consumers do not include pipeline. The kanban card cannot show a 'post-quote cadence running' badge, which is the visible payoff of the stage-bound trigger feature and the whole reason the seller trusts the automation fired.
- `sla.met` is consumed by leaderboard-secondary-metrics but `sla.breached` is not consumed by it. Asymmetric — you cannot compute a compliance percentage from successes alone.
- Attempt-ceiling reached is folded into `sequence.exited` with exit_reason. It deserves its own event: it is a DECISION prompt for the seller (recycle / cold / lost), not a silent termination, and no consumer can distinguish it from a normal completion without string-matching a reason field.
- `user.deactivated` triggers an admin prompt but no event is emitted for the orphaned work (`activity.orphaned` / `enrollment.orphaned`). Nothing drives an alert, a report, or a countdown — so in practice a departed seller's due follow-ups go dark exactly as the feature claims to prevent.
- `activity.reassigned` carries `activity_ids` and `enrollment_ids` as unbounded arrays. Offboarding a seller with 400 open activities produces an unusable payload. Emit a bulk_operation_id plus counts, and let consumers query.
- `activity.overdue` payload denormalizes `deal_value`, and `followup.gap_detected` denormalizes `deal_value`, `stage_id` and `product_type`. Pipeline owns those; carrying them in Tasks events guarantees drift between an alert and the card it points at. Carry opportunity_id and let consumers resolve.
- Calendar contract names are asserted, not agreed: meeting.booked / rescheduled / cancelled / no_show / completed. The committed research pattern was automation off 'appointment status changed'. If Calendar ships a single status-changed event, every one of these reactions is dead wiring. This needs to be an explicit cross-module contract note, not an assumption.
- No `activity.updated` event, so a due_at-neutral edit (subtype change, note added, outcome corrected by admin) never reaches the Lead 360 timeline that is supposed to be the single unified truth.

### 🚨 Silo violations (6)
- Supervisor 'may create a flagged coaching activity on a seller's lead' — a write into another seller's book. Even off by default, this is the first brick of the assignment engine the project explicitly excluded, and it creates the incoherent state of a role writing records against data it is elsewhere in this same spec forbidden to read.
- Supervisor 'Emergency pause: may stop a running cadence tenant-wide or for one seller (kill switch)'. To pause per-seller the UI must enumerate that seller's running enrollments, each of which is bound to a lead. Must be constrained to sequence-level or seller-level aggregate counts with no lead identifiers rendered, and that constraint must be written down.
- 'Drill into a breached SLA event' on the Team Follow-up Health screen. The sla.breached payload contains lead_id, lead_source and vendor_id. The screen's own stated principle is oversight 'by metric, not by browsing someone's leads' — the drill-in contradicts it unless the detail view is explicitly PII-free (source, vendor, elapsed, seller, timestamp only). Undefined today.
- Seller Speed-to-Lead dashboard exposes 'an anonymized team median for context'. That is a cross-seller derived statistic delivered to a seller outside the one sanctioned channel (the leaderboard). Either promote it into the public leaderboard as a named metric or remove it — ad-hoc anonymized cross-seller aggregates are how a silo erodes.
- SPEC CONTRADICTS THE VALIDATED MODEL IN THE OPPOSITE DIRECTION: this module repeatedly asserts supervisors cannot see leads ('cannot see or select another seller's leads', 'Overdue detail belongs to the seller's private book', 'lead-level rows' hidden). Phase 0 says supervisor AND admin have global visibility for oversight/reporting. This module has unilaterally invented a stricter supervisor tier than the client agreed to, and it will collide with every other module that assumes supervisor global read. Resolve at the product level and restate — do not let two modules ship different supervisor definitions.
- `user.deactivated` -> 'raise an admin prompt to reassign their open activities and enrollments' plus the bulk reassignment feature are the only assignment path, which is correctly flagged — but the guard is prose, not a rule. Needs a hard constraint: reassignment is permitted only when the source user is in deactivated/absent state, target is explicitly named by a human, no rule-based or automatic target selection, ever. Without that written as a constraint, the first 'can we auto-cover vacations?' request turns this into round-robin.

## Risks & dependencies
- ARCHITECTURAL DECISION REQUIRED: do we orchestrate SMS cadences ourselves and use Aloware only as transport (SMS API), or do we hand enrollment to Aloware Sequences (enroll/disenroll API)? Running both risks double-texting a prospect. Recommendation: WE own orchestration and timing (so auto-pause, quiet hours and pipeline-stage triggers stay in one brain), Aloware is transport + compliance layer only, and we never enroll a lead in an Aloware sequence for anything we also schedule.
- TCPA timing liability is ours, not Aloware's. Aloware provides DNC, 10DLC, STIR/SHAKEN and STOP handling, but WHEN a step is scheduled is our code. An incorrect lead time zone (ported mobile numbers make area codes unreliable) can produce an out-of-hours automated text. Mitigation: zip-first time-zone resolution, area code as fallback, tenant fallback last, hard block rather than best-effort, and the resolved zone stored on every activity for audit.
- Webhook matching is the highest-risk technical piece: mapping an Aloware call event to the right lead, seller and planned activity. Failure modes are duplicate logged calls, wrong-lead attribution, and phantom overdue rows. Mitigation: idempotency on the Aloware call id, deterministic phone->lead->owner resolution, an unmatched-events reconciliation queue visible to admins, and never silently discarding an unmatched call.
- The cadence engine needs a reliable scheduler (durable job queue with retries, backoff and clock-drift tolerance) inside a sub-$100/month infra budget. A cheap cron that misses a window turns a 60-second SLA into a 10-minute one. Mitigation: single scheduler with a tight tick for SLA-class work and a coarser tick for cadence steps; monitor scheduler lag as a first-class metric.
- Over-automation is an adoption risk: if sellers feel the system is texting their prospects behind their back, they disable it and we lose the whole module. Mitigation: calls are NEVER auto-dialed (agent-paced only), auto-send is opt-in per step, every enrollment shows a full send preview, and there is a per-lead and tenant-wide kill switch.
- The silo model has no coverage story. If a seller is sick for a week, their overdue follow-ups are invisible to everyone except through aggregate health metrics, and paid leads die. Mitigation: admin reassignment path + supervisor health alerting on backlog spikes. Explicit risk: this reassignment feature is the seed from which a lead-routing engine could grow — it must stay admin-only, bulk, audited and framed as book ownership transfer, or we violate the core architectural decision.
- Notification fatigue: SLA alerts, due-soon nudges, overdue pings and digests can collectively train sellers to ignore everything. Mitigation: only SLA-class events get interruptive push by default; everything else is in-app or digest.
- Dependency chain: lead-intake (lead.created, consent certificate, lead source/vendor, zip/time zone), pipeline (opportunity.stage_changed, closed_won/lost, stage config incl. which stages trigger cadences), calendar (meeting.booked/no_show/completed), comms (call/SMS/email send + Aloware webhooks), automations (action requests), notifications (delivery), reporting (rollups). If pipeline stage config or lead-source flags are not in place, the two highest-value automations (post-quote cadence and the SLA clock) cannot function.
- Priority scoring is a product risk, not just a technical one: if My Day's ordering feels wrong twice, sellers stop trusting it permanently. Mitigation: deterministic, explainable ordering with a visible 'why this is here' chip on every row, and no ML ranking in v1.
- CUT — deliberately excluded and recorded here: (a) a generic condition/branch workflow builder (we ship a short plain-English trigger vocabulary instead; a blank canvas is a named anti-pattern); (b) task assignment/delegation between sellers and shared team task queues (violates the silo model outright); (c) subtasks, dependencies, projects and checklists (project-management bloat, solves no seller problem); (d) gamified task points/badges (motivation belongs to the Leaderboard module; two competing scoreboards dilute both); (e) email open/click-triggered pauses (noisy and unreliable — only real replies pause); (f) ML best-time-to-call prediction (insufficient data at 50 sellers; configurable windows instead); (g) AI-generated follow-up copy per lead (post-MVP; templates first, and unreviewed AI text going to a 70-year-old prospect over SMS is a brand and compliance risk); (h) any predictive/auto-dialing of cadence call steps (TCPA abandonment exposure and explicitly against Aloware's own design).

## Open questions
- What is the actual first-contact SLA target the client wants — 60 seconds, 2 minutes, 5 minutes — and does it differ per lead vendor? The whole hot-lead UX (pin, countdown, interruptive push) is calibrated to this number.
- Should SLA compliance or 'activities completed' appear on the PUBLIC leaderboard as the secondary recognition category (the fairness mitigation for an all-time revenue board), or does the client want 'Most Improved' based on Earnings delta only? This changes what this module has to publish outside the silo.
- Do we orchestrate cadences ourselves with Aloware as transport, or delegate to Aloware Sequences? (See risks — this is the single biggest architectural decision in the module and needs a client/technical call before build.)
- Are SMS cadence steps auto-send by default, or does every outbound text require a seller tap? Auto-send maximizes speed; seller-approved maximizes trust and TCPA defensibility. Recommendation: auto-send allowed only for consented leads on admin-published templates, seller-approved everywhere else.
- Who covers a seller's My Day during PTO? Silos say nobody. If the client wants coverage, we need a temporary delegation concept — which is one small step from routing and needs an explicit decision.
- Can supervisors create coaching activities on a seller's lead without seeing the lead? It is high coaching value but the first crack in the silo wall.
- What is the maximum attempt policy per lead (dials per day, total touches) that the agency is comfortable defending under TCPA?
- Where do appointment reminders live — Calendar module or this one? They must be owned in exactly one place or prospects get two reminder texts. Recommendation: Calendar owns the meeting record, this module owns the reminder ACTIVITY, and Calendar never sends independently.
- What are the real FE/IUL milestone dates we can anchor persistency follow-ups on (first draft date, issue date, delivery date, free-look end)? These must exist as fields on the opportunity/policy or the post-sale cadence has nothing to schedule against.
- Do supervisors get access to call recordings and AI summaries for coaching, or is that tenant-configurable? Recording access has both coaching value and privacy/consent implications.
- Is there a policy-admin/carrier system we will eventually receive issue/lapse events from? If yes, the persistency cadence should be event-driven rather than date-anchored, which changes the design of the post-sale template.
- How should a paused-on-reply enrollment behave if the seller never responds — auto-resume after N days, or stay parked forever in the Waiting view? Auto-resume risks texting someone who was ignored; staying parked risks the lead dying silently.

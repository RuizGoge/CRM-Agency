# 8. Automations (Playbooks)

> **Module id:** `automations` · part of [`docs/02-functional-map.md`](../02-functional-map.md)

## Verdict
RESHAPE — keep the module, but stop calling it a "builder" and ship it as **Playbooks**: a closed, curated catalog of installable Life/FE/IUL recipes on top of a SHORT plain-English trigger vocabulary. Three reshapes are non-negotiable. (1) **Catalog-first, not canvas-first**: the primary surface is a template gallery ("Speed-to-lead first touch", "No-answer 5-day cadence", "No-show reschedule"); the guided When/Only-if/Then editor exists only to tune an installed playbook. A blank-canvas condition builder is the "simple to use, brutal to administer" anti-pattern and is CUT. (2) **This module ENFORCES compliance, it does not OWN it**: TCPA consent flags, DNC state, STOP/opt-out and A2P 10DLC throughput live in the intake/compliance + Aloware layer; Automations reads them and hard-blocks, and every block is logged and visible. Owning them here would duplicate the truth and create a legal liability with two sources. (3) **Absorb "Sequences/Cadences"** — there must NOT be a separate cadence module. A cadence is just a multi-step playbook with a wait step; splitting them produces two engines with two auto-pause rules and a lead getting texted twice. Also RESHAPE the scope boundary: Automations does NOT own the "lead is cold after 7 days" rule (Pipeline owns the staleness clock and emits the event) — Automations only reacts to it. Everything else in the brief survives the value test; the cut list is in risks/open_questions.

## Purpose & value
It makes sure a purchased Final Expense lead gets called and texted within seconds of landing — and then keeps every no-answer, post-quote, no-show and cold lead being followed up automatically, inside TCPA quiet hours and consent rules — so a seller stops losing deals to the follow-up they meant to do and forgot.

## Screens & states (9)

### My Playbooks (Automations home)
The seller's list of installed playbooks with on/off, what each one does in one line, how many of their leads are currently enrolled, when it last ran, and any failures needing attention.

**Key actions:** `Toggle a playbook on/off` · `Open the template gallery` · `Open a playbook to edit or view its steps` · `Open the live enrollment monitor` · `Pause all my automations (kill switch)` · `Fix a failed action from the alert strip`

- **Empty:** Headline: 'Your follow-up isn't running yet.' Body: 'Playbooks call, text and remind for you — the moment a lead lands, right after a no-answer, and when a deal goes quiet. Start with the one that matters most: reach a fresh lead in under a minute.' Primary button: 'Install Speed-to-lead first touch'. Secondary link: 'Browse all playbooks'. Footer note: 'Nothing sends until you turn it on. You can preview every message first.'
- **Loading:** Skeleton rows for the playbook cards (title bar, status pill, enrolled-count chip). The header count and the kill-switch stay disabled until loaded. No spinner-only screen.
- **Error:** Inline card: 'We couldn't load your playbooks. Your automations are still running normally in the background — this is a display problem.' with 'Try again' and a link to the status of the automation engine. If the engine itself is unreachable, the copy changes to: 'Automations are paused because the automation service is unreachable. Nothing is being sent. We'll resume automatically and no steps will be lost.'
- **No permission:** Not reachable for a seller (every seller owns their workspace). For a supervisor viewing another seller: read-only banner 'Viewing Maria Alvarez's playbooks — read only. You can pause them, but only Maria or an admin can edit them.'
- **Mobile:** Cards stack full-width; toggle is the primary tap target; the enrollment monitor and gallery collapse into a bottom tab. The 'Pause all my automations' control stays reachable in one tap from the top bar — this is the screen a seller opens in a panic.

- **Empty:** Only possible under a filter: 'No playbooks match this filter.' Body: 'Try clearing the product filter, or start from the ones every agent installs first.' Button: 'Show all playbooks'.
- **Loading:** Grid of 6 skeleton tiles with category headers already rendered, so the shape of the catalog is visible immediately.
- **Error:** 'We couldn't load the playbook catalog.' Body: 'Your installed playbooks are unaffected.' Button: 'Try again'.
- **No permission:** Sellers always have access to install into their own workspace. If the admin has locked the catalog: 'Your admin manages playbooks for the team. ## Typical automations
- When a new lead lands from a vendor feed → put them at the top of my Power Dialer list, create a 'Call now' task, notify me, and text them within a minute (if consent is on file and it's not quiet hours).
- When a call ends with no answer → wait 20 minutes, send a text; if still nothing, try again tomorrow at a different time of day, for 5 days.
- When a card moves into Quoted → send the quote recap text now, a follow-up text in 2 business days, and create a call task on day 4.
- When an appointment is booked → stop all prospecting cadences, confirm now, remind 24 hours before and 1 hour before, in the lead's local time.
- When an appointment is a no-show → text 'sorry we missed you' with a reschedule prompt immediately, create a same-day call task, and try twice more before returning them to normal follow-up.
- When a lead goes cold after 7 days without activity → send one low-pressure re-engagement message and create a task; never more than 3 nudges in the lead's lifetime.
- When a lead replies by text or email → pause every playbook they're in and put a reply task at the top of My Day.
- When a lead texts STOP → cancel every enrollment on every channel, mark them suppressed, and never enroll them again.
- When a deal is marked Closed-Won → stop all sales follow-up, send a welcome message, remind me before the first premium draft date, and ask for a referral after the free-look period.
- When it's a client's birthday → send a personal-sounding message from their agent and create an optional call task.
- When a policy hits its anniversary → for IUL, create a task to refresh the illustration and review funding; for Final Expense, send a check-in and confirm the beneficiary.
- When an application sits in Underwriting too long → notify me with the outstanding requirements and text the client a reminder.
- When an automated message fails permanently → stop the cadence, tell me why, and flag the bad phone number or email on the lead.
- When a new seller is created → auto-install the agency's default playbooks so their follow-up runs from day one.
- When an admin flips the emergency stop → hold every queued message across the tenant, show a banner, and resume without firing the backlog as a burst.

## KPIs
- Median speed-to-lead: lead.created → first outbound attempt (call enqueued or SMS dispatched). Target < 60 seconds; this is the module's headline number.
- % of new leads receiving a first automated touch within 5 minutes (target > 90% of consent-eligible leads).
- Automation adoption: % of active sellers with at least one enabled playbook, and % with speed-to-lead specifically enabled.
- Automation-attributed appointments booked (meeting.scheduled within the attribution window of an automation.step_executed).
- No-show recovery rate: % of meeting.no_show that result in a rescheduled meeting within 7 days.
- Cold-lead recovery rate: % of lead.went_cold that produce a reply, meeting or stage advance after a nudge.
- Automation-attributed Closed-Won count and attributed Earnings (annual premium), reported under both first-touch and last-touch attribution.
- Reply rate and answer rate per playbook and per message template.
- Auto-pause correctness: % of enrollments that exit within 60 seconds of a lead reply or booking (target ~100%; anything below is a trust-destroying defect).
- STOP / opt-out rate per message template (compliance canary — alert above the tenant threshold, typically 2–3%).
- Consent blocks per 100 attempted automated messages (should trend toward zero; a rising number means intake is capturing consent badly, not that the gate is wrong).
- Quiet-hours deferral volume and average deferral length (a proxy for timezone data quality).
- Failed action rate and dead-letter queue depth / age.
- Duplicate-suppression hits (idempotency collisions caught) — proves webhook redelivery is not double-messaging leads.
- Playbook install-to-enable-to-still-enabled-after-30-days funnel (catches templates that look good and get switched off).

"Genuinely Life/FE/IUL-specific — and none of it is hard-coded into the engine. (1) SPEED-TO-LEAD AS A HARD REQUIREMENT: purchased Final Expense leads arrive via ping-post and are sold to multiple agents; they decay in minutes. That is why this module runs the first touch on the event hot path (target < 60s) instead of a batch scheduler — a generic CRM's 5-minute polling automation would be functionally useless here. (2) TCPA AS AN EXECUTION-TIME GATE, NOT A CHECKBOX: automated dialing and texting of US consumers is the regulated act. Consent state, the lead-vendor CONSENT CERTIFICATE captured at intake, DNC/suppression and STOP propagation are re-read at every send, and quiet hours are computed in the LEAD's local timezone (8:00am–9:00pm default). This is also why we route dialing through Aloware's agent-paced Power Dialer and ship NO predictive dialing action — abandonment-rate exposure. (3) FE DEMOGRAPHIC CADENCE SHAPE: senior buyers under-answer unknown numbers and over-read SMS, so the shipped ladders are call → voicemail → text → email with different time-of-day bands per attempt, and messages are short and plain. (4) FE-SPECIFIC POST-SALE MOMENTS: policy delivery and the FIRST PREMIUM DRAFT DATE — the lapse-and-chargeback cliff — get their own reminder playbook, which has no analogue in a generic sales CRM. (5) IUL-SPECIFIC MOMENTS: illustration delivered → follow-up, and the annual review / illustration refresh on the policy anniversary, because IUL is a multi-year funded product, not a one-shot close. (6) LOSS-REASON-AWARE WIN-BACK: win-back only fires for price/affordability and no-funds, never for 'doesn't qualify (health/age)' — re-soliciting a declined applicant is both useless and offensive. HOW THE CORE STAYS VERTICAL-AGNOSTIC: the engine knows only tenant_id, lead, opportunity, stage, activity, channel, consent_status and timezone. Everything insurance-specific lives in DATA, not code — the shipped playbooks are rows in automation_template_catalog with a product_hint, 'annual premium' is just the configured deal_value field referenced by a merge tag, and 'policy anniversary' is a date field on the record that emits a generic policy.anniversary_reached. Stage-bound triggers reference configurable stage_ids, never stage names. Adding a second vertical (e.g. Medicare or P&C) means seeding a new template pack and new date-field triggers — zero engine changes. The only compliance behavior that is hard-coded is US-jurisdictional (TCPA quiet hours, opt-out), which is correct: it is a property of the market, not of the vertical."

## ⚔️ Adversarial review (sales-ops critic)

**Verdict:** NEEDS WORK — the engine skeleton (versions, pinning, idempotency, gate chain, kill switches) is competent, but it is still an automation PLATFORM rather than an FE/IUL floor tool: it lets an automation close deals into the public leaderboard, keys consent and frequency caps to lead_id instead of phone (which breaks the moment ping-post sells the same senior to two sellers in the same agency), has no answer at all for an offline seller holding a lead that decays in minutes, and ships automated dialing plus voicemail drop that our non-native Aloware integration cannot legally or technically execute.

### Must fix — missing for real operation (14)
- SELLER UNAVAILABILITY — the single biggest hole. In a silo model with no routing, a ping-post FE lead landing at 8:47pm on a seller who is off is simply never called, and it cost the owner $35-$60. There is no presence/shift model, no 'fresh lead unworked for N minutes' escalation, no supervisor alert, and no explicit manual-reassignment path. Without it, someone WILL bolt on round-robin and break the silo.
- lead.owner_changed and user.deactivated are not consumed. Manual reassignment is the only permitted transfer in this product, and when an agent quits, their 300 leads move. Enrollments, pinned timers, power-dialer list entries and 'Call now' tasks all still point at owner_seller_id of a disabled user — automated texts keep going out signed by an agent who no longer works there.
- State-level calling/texting law. The guardrail is one federal 8:00am-9:00pm window, tenant-narrowable. A nationwide FE floor needs a state rule table: FL and OK mini-TCPA (8-8 plus per-subject call-frequency limits), state Sunday/holiday restrictions, and the rule that the NARROWEST of federal/state/tenant wins. One window is a lawsuit waiting on the calendar.
- Frequency caps, opt-out and do-not-automate are all keyed on lead_id. Ping-post sells the same consumer to multiple buyers; inside one agency two sellers can each hold that person in their own book. Caps and STOP must be keyed on E.164 phone at TENANT scope, or the same senior gets two cadences from the same 10DLC brand and hits STOP on both.
- Fail-open vs fail-closed is never stated. When the consent/DNC/Aloware lookup times out, the engine must FAIL CLOSED and defer, and that must be written into the spec, not left to implementation.
- One-off scheduled callback ('call me back Thursday at 3'). This is the most-used automation on a real FE floor and there is no per-lead snooze / disposition-driven single scheduled callback — only multi-step playbooks.
- Chargeback and persistency triggers: NSF / failed first draft, not-taken-out, lapse, policy issued/delivered. The spec references policy delivery and first draft date but no module produces policy state; policy.anniversary_reached has no declared producer. Either declare seller-entered policy status fields on the opportunity or cut the post-sale playbooks.
- A declared producer for date-based triggers. The spec bans cron, then ships birthday, policy anniversary, stall threshold and cold-lead triggers — all of which are date sweeps. Name the daily scheduler service, its lead-local date semantics, and who owns it.
- Per-vendor playbook variants. Consent language, lead quality and acceptable aggressiveness differ per lead vendor; lead_source is only a filter value. The speed-to-lead copy for vendor A is not legally interchangeable with vendor B.
- SMS/dialer spend meter per seller and per tenant. Automated SMS is pass-through cost against a sub-$100/month infra target; there is a segment counter in the template editor and no spend anywhere else.
- sms.delivered and carrier-filtering handling. Advancing an enrollment on 'accepted by provider' is not delivery; 10DLC carrier filtering (30007-class) looks like success and the cadence marches on while nothing reaches the lead.
- Timezone resolution data. 'State/ZIP with area-code fallback' is wrong for mobile-only FE demographics (a 415 area code living in Tampa). Needs a ZIP->TZ table and, when unknown, a conservative intersection window (e.g. 10am-7pm ET/PT overlap) — never the seller's timezone.
- A stored speed-to-lead measurement (first_touch_latency_seconds on the lead, dial vs SMS separately). The supervisor health view claims median speed-to-lead per seller with no field that owns the number.
- Compliance evidence export is marked mvp_candidate:false while mass automated SMS ships in MVP. Shipping the liability without the defense is the wrong order — promote it to MVP.

### Too generic / boilerplate (9)
- The 'Guided When / Only if / Then editor' + ANDed typed filters + priority + exclusivity groups + versioning-with-diff is a generic marketing-automation engine chapter lifted from any SaaS spec. For an FE/IUL floor, three playbooks carry ~90% of the value (speed-to-lead, no-answer ladder, no-show recovery). Ship those three with tunable timings/copy only; seller-authored custom playbooks do not exist in MVP.
- Merge-tag set (first_name, seller_first_name, agency_name, appointment_time_local, quoted_premium, product_type) is CRM boilerplate. A first automated SMS to a PURCHASED FE lead must carry the consent anchor: {vendor_brand} (the site/brand they actually filled out), {lead_request_date}, agent name + resident license number, and the state-required disclosure. That sentence is the TCPA defense; without those tags the whole compliance story is decoration.
- 'It is a lead's birthday' is pure CRM boilerplate. On an FE floor the date trigger that moves money is AGE-NEAREST-BIRTHDAY on an OPEN lead — 'rates go up in 21 days, call now' — not a happy-birthday text to a 74-year-old prospect who has not bought.
- Playbook Performance with a first-touch vs last-touch attribution toggle is analytics theater for a 50-seller single-agency tenant. The floor needs: enrolled -> contacted -> appointment set -> Closed-Won count, plus STOP rate per template. Attribution modeling is a slide, not a decision.
- 'Adoption recommendations' ('You had 34 no-answer calls last week…') is SaaS growth-hacking bolted onto an insurance CRM. Org auto-install of default playbooks on user.created already solves adoption for a 50-seat single agency.
- install_count as gallery 'social proof' is generic marketplace UX. Inside one agency with one admin publishing approved copy, nobody installs based on popularity.
- 'Closed-Won welcome and referral ask' is generic. The FE version is first-premium DRAFT DATE confirmation + beneficiary confirmation + free-look expectation — because the agent's chargeback risk lives in the first draft, not in referral volume.
- The trigger 'A call ends with no answer' is generic because it hides the real object: Aloware dispositions are tenant-configurable strings. The spec needs an admin-owned mapping table from Aloware disposition -> semantic bucket (no answer, busy, voicemail, wrong number, DNC request, not interested, callback scheduled, appointment set). Without it, every tenant's no-answer cadence misfires.
- 'Multi-channel fallback ladder' framed as call -> voicemail -> SMS -> email is generic cadence-vendor copy. We are not an Aloware native partner: there is no server-initiated dial without a live agent leg, so this ladder cannot execute as written.

### Bloat to cut (12)
- Seller-authored custom playbooks. The verdict says the editor 'exists only to tune an installed playbook', then the Playbook Editor ships a brand-new-custom-playbook empty state ('Start with when it should happen'). Contradiction — cut authoring to admin only in MVP.
- Rule version diff viewer and one-click revert. Keep immutable versions + in-flight pinning (that is a correctness requirement); the diff/revert UI is a developer tool nobody on a phone floor opens.
- Dry run / 'Preview impact' projecting 7-day message volume and consent-block counts. It requires re-running the full gate chain over the entire book. Replace with an activation-time enrollment scope cap ('only leads created in the last 30 days', hard max N) plus the existing rate smoothing — same blast protection, a fraction of the machinery.
- First-touch vs last-touch attribution toggle on Playbook Performance.
- The adoption-recommendations engine.
- enroll_aloware_sequence / disenroll_aloware_sequence as step types. The module's own verdict forbids two cadence engines with two auto-pause rules, then hands the lead to Aloware's sequence engine — whose auto-pause we cannot observe and whose disenroll we must race on reply. Keep Power Dialer enqueue; cut sequence delegation.
- notify_supervisor as a step available inside a seller's own playbook. Nobody builds it, and it turns a personal cadence into an escalation channel with no hierarchy model behind it.
- The automated call leg and the pre-recorded voicemail drop inside the multi-channel fallback ladder. Prerecorded/artificial-voice messages need prior express written consent and are a live litigation magnet, and calling it 'agent-initiated' while encoding it as an unattended automation step is self-contradictory. The legitimate version is a task with a one-tap agent-triggered voicemail drop.
- Four overlapping suppression mechanisms — priority, exclusivity groups, per-lead frequency caps, cross-playbook cap, throttle, and queue smoothing. Collapse to ONE ordered gate chain (do-not-automate/suppression -> consent -> quiet hours + state rules -> per-number cap -> queue priority) with a single skip-reason taxonomy. Six ways to not send a text is six ways to debug why nothing went out.
- Six funnel-moment categories in the gallery for roughly twelve templates. One flat list, product filter only.
- The birthday touch as specified (replace with the age-change urgency trigger on open leads).
- The Playbook Performance screen's 'duplicate the winning playbook / retire the losing one' workflow — with admin-only authoring and a curated catalog there is nothing to duplicate.

### Event gaps (14)
- Commands are dressed as events. sms.send_requested, email.send_requested, activity.create_requested, powerdialer.enqueue_requested, opportunity.stage_change_requested and lead.field_update_requested are commands. Each needs a paired result the engine CONSUMES (sms.accepted / sms.rejected, activity.created, opportunity.stage_change_rejected, powerdialer.enqueue_failed) or the enrollment state machine never closes the loop and 'executed' means 'we shouted into the bus'.
- sms.sent / sms.delivered are not consumed at all — only sms.delivery_failed. Steps advance on optimism, which is exactly the 'silent non-delivery manufactures false confidence' failure the spec claims to prevent.
- Exit rules name 'answered inbound call' as a pause trigger, but no inbound-answered event is consumed. Only call.completed and call.missed exist. An inbound call the seller ANSWERS is the strongest engagement signal in the product and it does not pause anything.
- lead.opted_out is keyed to a lead. It must be contact.opted_out keyed on phone_e164 + channel at tenant scope, or an opt-out in seller A's book does not protect seller B's identical contact.
- No lead.owner_changed, no user.deactivated, no automation.enrollment_transferred. Reassignment is the only permitted lead movement in this architecture and the event does not exist.
- No consumption of the Aloware transcript / AloAi call-summary webhooks (call.transcription_ready, aloai.summary_ready) despite those being the contracted integration's highest-value payloads. They also arrive AFTER call.completed, so a no-answer cadence keyed on call.completed races the enrichment.
- opportunity.stalled, policy.anniversary_reached and lead.birthday_reached have no declared producer, and automation_guardrail has no per-stage stall-threshold fields even though the underwriting-stalled feature calls the threshold 'configurable'.
- 'compliance' is listed as an expected consumer of automation.blocked_by_consent, but the module's own verdict says consent lives in intake/contacts-leads + the Aloware layer. Phantom consumer — name the real one.
- automation.rule_disabled carries affected_enrollment_count but emits no per-enrollment automation.enrollment_paused. Eighty lead timelines silently show nothing, which is precisely the black box the run-log feature exists to prevent.
- The kill-switch resume backlog decision (discard vs re-spread) produces no per-enrollment event or log row, so there is no record of which follow-ups were deliberately dropped.
- Exclusivity supersession is folded into automation.enrollment_canceled with cancel_reason, but the payload never names the WINNING rule — 'recording why' is claimed in the feature and absent from the schema.
- sms.send_requested carries consent_reference_id, but automation_action_log has no consent_reference_id or to_phone_e164 column. The evidence chain breaks exactly where the evidence pack reads it: the log must snapshot the consent proof as of send time, not point at a mutable consent record.
- No automation.speed_to_lead_missed (or equivalent SLA-breach event) even though the supervisor health view and the whole ping-post value proposition are built on that number.
- automation.throttled has an action_taken field, but automation_action_log's outcome enum collapses deferred-by-cap and dropped-by-cap into a single 'throttled' value — the two have opposite consequences for the lead.

### 🚨 Silo violations (7)
- automation_outbound_queue is tenant-wide with only priority_class and no per-seller fair share. Seller A's cold-lead batch consumes the tenant's A2P throughput and delays Seller B's speed-to-lead text — one seller's automation directly damages another seller's leaderboard position. Add a per-seller quota inside each priority class.
- Cross-seller opt-out and per-number cap suppression is legally mandatory, but the module's signature feature is plain-English skip reasons on the timeline. 'Text not sent — this contact opted out on Feb 3' leaks the existence of another agent's lead. Skip text must be brand-scoped and non-attributive ('this number opted out of messages from our agency'), and no enrollment or log row may reference the other seller's lead_id, seller_id or playbook.
- Org-scope rules carry owner_seller_id = NULL and the spec never states that the resulting enrollment materializes under the LEAD'S OWNING SELLER. An org playbook with an enqueue_power_dialer step and no per-seller list identity is a centralized dispatcher in disguise — the exact routing engine the architecture forbids.
- move_stage is in the action catalog with no prohibition on Closed stages. Combined with set_field (which can populate annual premium), an automation can move an opportunity into Closed-Won, mint Earnings, and re-rank the PUBLIC leaderboard with no human involved. Required-field gating does not stop this. move_stage must be hard-blocked server-side for any stage flagged closed (won or lost) — leaderboard integrity is the one thing every seller in the tenant can see.
- Automation-created activities and 'Call now' tasks are not flagged is_automated. Any activity-based leaderboard metric or the 'Most Improved' secondary recognition category can then be inflated by installing more playbooks rather than by working leads.
- The speed-to-lead escalation gap (offline seller holding a decaying purchased lead) is the exact seam where round-robin fallback will be smuggled in. The spec must state explicitly that escalation is notify + supervisor alert + MANUAL reassignment, never automatic transfer of ownership.
- install_count and gallery social proof are the only aggregates that legitimately cross seller boundaries; the spec should declare them intentional non-lead aggregates rather than leaving a reviewer to assume a leak.

## Risks & dependencies
- HARD DEPENDENCY on event-time accuracy: speed-to-lead only works if lead.created is emitted synchronously at ingest. If the intake module batches or polls its lead source, this module's headline KPI is unachievable no matter how fast the engine is. Intake must push, not poll.
- HARD DEPENDENCY on Aloware: SMS dispatch, DNC suppression, TCPA pre-send gating, STOP handling, A2P 10DLC registration and Power Dialer enqueue all live there. If the Aloware token, 10DLC brand/campaign, or webhook delivery is not in place, most of this module cannot execute — only tasks, notifications and internal steps would work. The module must degrade to that mode explicitly (with a visible banner) rather than fail silently.
- Webhook redelivery from Aloware is expected, not exceptional. Without the idempotency key on (rule_version_id, lead_id, trigger_event_id) enforced as a DB unique constraint, duplicate texts WILL ship. This is a correctness dependency, not a nice-to-have.
- Timezone data quality is the weak link in quiet-hours compliance. State/ZIP is usually present on purchased leads but not guaranteed; area-code inference is unreliable for mobile numbers. Mitigation: default to the most conservative window when timezone is unknown, and surface unknown-timezone leads to the seller. Residual legal risk remains and should be reviewed by counsel.
- Scheduling infrastructure cost vs. the sub-$100/month target: a durable delayed-job store with second-level precision on the speed-to-lead path plus millions of low-priority scheduled steps is the most likely budget-buster in the whole product. Needs a deliberate design decision in Phase 5 (e.g. a single hot queue for time-critical steps plus a cheap cron sweep for long waits).
- Silo model + org templates is a genuine tension: admins need standardized compliant copy, sellers need autonomy. Getting the fork/lock split wrong produces either uncontrolled message copy under the agency's 10DLC brand or a seller revolt against 'the admin's texts'.
- Attribution of Closed-Won Earnings to a playbook is inherently contested. Two attribution models are shown side by side precisely because a single number will be argued with, and because leaderboard Earnings must NEVER be modified by this module — Automations only reads and reports, Pipeline owns the money.
- Over-automation is the reputational risk that kills the product: too many touches → STOP → a burned phone number and a degraded 10DLC reputation shared by the whole team. Frequency caps, exclusivity groups, the STOP-rate canary and lifetime nudge caps exist to bound this and must not be cut as 'polish'.
- CUT — blank-canvas visual flow builder with branching nodes and boolean condition trees. Fails the value test: no insurance closer will author one, it makes the module unsupportable, and it is the exact 'brutal to administer' anti-pattern we rejected.
- CUT — arbitrary outbound webhooks / Zapier-style actions. Turns an auditable CRM into an unauditable integration platform, and creates a data-exfiltration path out of the per-seller silos.
- CUT — A/B testing of message variants. With ~50 sellers and per-seller books, no single playbook reaches statistical significance in a useful timeframe; it would produce confident nonsense. Revisit if tenant volume grows.
- CUT — AI-generated message copy per lead at send time. Unreviewable copy going out under an agency's regulated 10DLC campaign is a compliance liability; AI is acceptable for SUGGESTING template copy a human approves, not for generating what ships.
- CUT — broadcast/ringless voicemail drops and any bulk blast to a purchased list. TCPA exposure with no per-lead consent chain.
- CUT — predictive send-time optimization. Requires data we will not have for a long time and directly conflicts with the deterministic, explainable quiet-hours behavior sellers need to trust.
- CUT — seller-editable quiet hours. Compliance floors are not a per-user preference; only an admin may narrow them.

## Open questions
- Who owns and emits lead.birthday_reached and policy.anniversary_reached? These are date-based scans, not reactions. Proposal: a shared scheduler capability that emits them, with contacts-leads owning the date fields — Automations should only consume. Needs a ruling from the integration-map pass so we do not end up with a cron inside every module.
- Event-name reconciliation needed across modules: I consume lead.went_cold and opportunity.stalled (both assumed emitted by Pipeline) and lead.opted_out / lead.consent_updated (assumed emitted by the intake/compliance + Aloware layer). If Pipeline instead names its staleness event opportunity.went_cold, this module's trigger catalog must be updated. Same for call.completed vs. call.disposition_recorded on the Aloware side — I standardized on call.completed carrying a disposition field.
- Command-vs-fact event style: I emit *_requested command events (sms.send_requested, opportunity.stage_change_requested) so the owning module stays the authority and can reject. If the architecture instead prefers direct service calls for commands and events only for facts, this list should be halved. Needs one project-wide decision.
- Does an automated SMS reset the 7-day cold clock? If yes, an active cold-lead nudge makes a lead permanently 'warm' and the staleness signal becomes meaningless. Strong recommendation: only HUMAN activity and INBOUND lead activity reset the clock; automated outbound does not. Pipeline must implement this, not Automations.
- Should automation-created tasks count as 'activity' for the leaderboard/dashboards? Recommendation: no — otherwise a seller could top the activity metrics by installing playbooks and never touching a phone.
- How much of the cadence should run in OUR engine vs. Aloware Sequences? Aloware sequences are cheaper to build on and carry native compliance, but the state lives outside our timeline and our auto-pause rules, which risks the exact drift-prone third-party snapshot anti-pattern. Current recommendation: our engine owns orchestration and exit rules; Aloware executes single sends. Needs validation against real Aloware API rate limits.
- A2P 10DLC brand/campaign structure: one campaign for the whole agency, or per-seller numbers? This determines whether one seller's bad template degrades everyone's deliverability, and therefore how aggressive the STOP-rate kill threshold must be. Client/Aloware question.
- On resume after a kill switch: discard the backlog or re-spread it? Discarding loses real follow-ups; re-spreading risks a delayed blast. Proposal is to ask at resume time with a default of 'discard anything older than 24 hours', but this needs a product ruling.
- Do supervisors need the ability to enroll a lead in another seller's book? I ruled no (it violates the silo's spirit — outreach under a seller's name that they did not initiate). Confirm with the client, because a coaching-heavy agency may want it.
- Confirm the exact quiet-hours default the agency wants. TCPA's floor is 8:00am–9:00pm lead-local; several carriers and best practice suggest 9:00am–8:00pm. Recommend shipping the narrower window as the default and letting the admin widen only to the legal floor.
- Should the 'Preview impact' dry run be MVP after all? I marked it non-MVP, but the first time a seller enables a cold-lead nudge over a 400-lead dormant book without it, we will get an opt-out spike. A cheap fallback for MVP: hard-cap first-day enrollment volume per newly enabled playbook.
